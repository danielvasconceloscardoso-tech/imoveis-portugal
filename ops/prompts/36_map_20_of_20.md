# Prompt 36: the map, from 12/20 to 20/20

CEO, 27 Oct 2026. Decision D62. The operator loves the map, but today it is "a 12, and I want a 20/20". His image: Kevin De Bruyne at Chelsea (great talent, badly used) against De Bruyne at City (the centre of everything).

The map is our best player. Build the system around it. This replaces prompt 35 §3.7 (the place card).

**The brief:** more beautiful, more captivating, and when you click, nothing may feel like a Google form. Everything is direct and physical: you touch the country, the country answers.

Build on `design-v2` as "map v3". The frozen v1 stays untouched, and map-v2's useful parts (time slider, compare, golf, coastal glide) are folded in. You decide the details. Send recordings, not questions.

## 1. The look: a country you want to touch

1. **Relief.** Portugal as a sculpted 3D landscape:
   - real terrain with soft hillshade, from an open elevation source with a commercial licence (for example Copernicus DEM or the public AWS Terrain Tiles; credited on /credits and in the map's small attribution);
   - the coastline as a fine bright line;
   - the Atlantic with a subtle depth gradient;
   - a soft atmospheric haze at the horizon;
   - fixed golden-hour light from the southwest, so the relief casts gentle shadows.
2. **Our own basemap,** nothing that looks like Google Maps: sea, land, relief, coastline, rivers (Tejo, Douro), place names in Playfair (big places) and Manrope (the rest), labels appearing with zoom.
3. **Data that looks crafted, not blocky:**
   - the price view becomes either refined translucent columns with soft light, or a luminous colour field draped on the relief: your call, the more beautiful one;
   - one tuned colour ramp, green to warm sand, tested for colour blindness;
   - a legend that is one thin line with two numbers.
4. **Night mode, the signature moment:** a "Day / Night" switch. At night the land goes dark and Portugal's lights glow, from NASA Black Marble night-lights imagery (public domain; check the licence and record it). Lisbon, Porto, the Algarve coast and Madeira shine. It is the screenshot people share and the frame for the film.
5. **60 fps** on a mid-range phone, with a still fallback for reduced motion and weak devices.

## 2. The play: direct, physical, one gesture at a time

1. **Budget is a gesture, not a field.** A beautiful slider or dial in the reader's currency, with "tap to type" for an exact figure. As it moves, the places it reaches glow, and one live sentence answers, for example: "Around 1.5 M€ buys about 290 m² in Grândola, 160 m² in Cascais." It uses the existing m² logic and medians, labelled as medians.
2. **Hover and touch.** On hover, a place lifts slightly and shows its name and one number. On click or tap, the camera flies in smoothly (about 1.2 s, eased) and the place card slides in:
   - from the side on desktop, from the bottom on mobile, Apple Maps style;
   - the card holds a photograph, the name, three numbers (price per m², 10-year change, rent), the 10-year line, and two actions: "See homes here" and "Talk to the concierge";
   - "Compare" and the details are one tap further.
3. **Search like Spotlight.** A magnifier, plus ⌘K on desktop. Type "Comp" and the map flies to Comporta. It also finds parishes, golf courses, beaches and partner developments.
4. **Layers as quiet chips** with thin icons: Price · 10 years · Rent · Yield · Golf · Beaches (OpenStreetMap `natural=beach`, ODbL) · New developments (partners only). One active at a time, plus golf and beaches as overlays.
5. **Time.** A slim scrubber from 2016 to 2026 with a play button. Press play and ten years pass in about 12 seconds: the country breathes, Porto and the coast rise.
6. **Compare.** Pin two places and a split card shows their numbers side by side, mirrored.
7. **Tours, the captivating part.** Three guided camera flights, each about 20 seconds with one-line captions, which the reader can take over at any moment:
   - "The coast, from Porto to Comporta";
   - "Golf on the Algarve";
   - "Madeira".

   A fourth, "Ten years in twenty seconds", plays the time-lapse from above.
8. **Arrival.**
   - On the home page: the slow rotation, stopping after 15 seconds or at the first interaction (prompt 34 §2).
   - On the map page: a single opening flight from the Atlantic onto the coast, then still.
   - Every state (place, layer, year, budget) is a shareable link.

9. **Golf data: our own list, complete** (operator, 28 Oct: the Comporta and Algarve courses are public and well known; task 33d found them missing from OpenStreetMap).
   - OpenStreetMap is only a starting point. Build `content/facts/golf/courses.csv` covering every course in the Algarve, Comporta and Tróia, Lisbon and Cascais, Madeira and the North.
   - Columns: name, resort, municipality, holes, designer, opening year, official website, latitude, longitude, source URL, date checked.
   - Sources: the Federação Portuguesa de Golfe course list, each course's or resort's official website, Visit Portugal or Visit Algarve golf pages, and the developers' own releases (for example Pinheirinho by VIC, Dunas at JNcQUOI Club Comporta, Ombria).
   - Coordinates come from the official address, geocoded with OpenStreetMap Nominatim, or from coordinates the resort publishes. Never copy them from Google Maps.
   - Courses with an OpenStreetMap outline are drawn as their shape. The others appear as an elegant pin with the same card (name, holes, designer, year, "See homes nearby").
   - Courses still under construction are marked "opening 2028" (or their date), never shown as open.
   - The golf premium (prompt 33 §4.2) uses this list to choose the parishes. Porches and any doubtful outline are checked against the official source before they appear in a chart.

## 3. Mobile first

- Full-screen map with a bottom sheet that rests at three heights (peek, half, full), the way Apple Maps works.
- Controls within thumb reach.
- No hover-only features.

## 4. Never

- input boxes in a grid;
- default selects or checkboxes;
- a toolbar of many buttons;
- tables in the first view;
- popups that cover the map;
- spinners longer than 300 ms without a placeholder;
- instant jumps where a smooth move belongs;
- anything that looks like Google Maps, Material Design or a form.
