# Prompt 60: the software we can build on our data, who has done it, and what the site must change now (due Fri 6 Nov 2026)

CEO, 2 Nov 2026, 23:10. Operator: think bigger. The media business pays the bills; the data we collect may be worth more than the media itself, and it changes what a buyer pays at exit (D76). Find the products, the companies that already did it, the money, and what the site must start capturing **now** so the data exists in 12 months. **One memo in English, max 8 pages, plus a one-page table** (rule 15). Options with numbers; the operator decides.

## 1. The data we will hold (inventory first)
1. What we collect or can collect, with consent: quiz answers (region, lifestyle, budget, who comes), Concierge requests (where, budget, timing, financing, country of origin), searches and map use (regions, layers, price filters), letter behaviour (opens, clicks per topic and region), developer listing views and requests, sponsor engagement, desafio answers.
2. What we compile from official sources: INE prices, the index pipeline, schools, health, the cast and places bank.
3. **The qualified-lead profile (operator, 2 Nov):** for each buyer who asks, with consent, we know when they plan to buy, the budget, the origin country, the regions they look at and match, and which partners they need (bank, lawyer, tax, insurance, schools, health). That is a full picture of the buyer's journey that no portal holds. Map every field, how we collect it today and what is missing.
4. What is unique: no one else sees **foreign and diaspora buyer intent before the purchase**, by region, budget and origin country. Say whether that is true, with evidence.

## 2. The products (rank by revenue per hour of human time and by exit value)
For each: what it is, who pays, how much, comparable companies and their prices or revenue, build effort with our stack, legal limits.
1. **Demand intelligence for developers:** what foreign buyers want, where, at what budget, before they build or price a project (a dashboard or a quarterly report).
2. **Market reports for banks, insurers, funds and municipalities:** foreign demand by region and origin; the annual "who buys Portugal from abroad".
3. **A pricing and absorption tool** for developers (how fast units sell to foreign buyers by type and price).
4. **An API or data licence** for portals, banks and proptechs.
5. **SaaS for developers:** the digital sales room we already build for partners (listing, availability, map, request flow, monthly report) sold as software per project.
6. **Lead intelligence, within D6 and D14:** what the qualified-lead data is worth without selling leads or charging per lead:
   - aggregated demand and timing signals sold to developers, banks and insurers ("312 buyers from Luxembourg and France plan to buy in Porto e Norte in the next 6 months, median budget X");
   - the matching and scoring software (buyer to region, to development, to partner) as a product and as an exit asset;
   - partner sponsorship priced on the quality of the audience it reaches, flat (D6), with the reader's consent naming each partner;
   - what a buyer (portal, bank, proptech) pays for a qualified, consented buyer pipeline at exit: comparables and multiples.
   Flag, do not decide: any model that would need D6 or D14 to change goes to the operator as a question.
7. Anything else the research finds (valuation inputs, relocation tools, municipality dashboards).

## 3. Learn from those who did it
Study, with sources (annual reports, funding rounds, interviews, pricing pages): Casafari, Idealista/data, Confidencial Imobiliário (SIR), Imovirtual, Zillow (Zestimate and its data business), CoStar, Rightmove's data services, PropertyData (UK), Reonomy, Placer.ai, Similarweb, Morning Brew and The Information (media to data/products), Nomad List (community to data). For each: what data, what product, who pays, price, revenue or valuation, and the one move we should copy.

## 4. Legal frame (cite articles)
1. GDPR: aggregate and anonymise; consent wording and purpose for analytics and product use (arts. 5, 6, 7, 13, 89; Recital 26); never sell personal data or per-lead (D6).
2. Lead data specifically: consent per partner (art. 7), joint controllership with partners (art. 26), retention; the line between advertising and acting as a credit intermediary (DL 81-C/2017), insurance distributor (Lei 7/2019) or real-estate mediator (Lei 15/2013) when we pass a buyer to a bank, insurer or the agency.
3. Database right and contracts (DL 122/2000), INE and official data licences, OSM ODbL share-alike.
4. Link with prompt 58: software licence income and the IP box (CIRC art. 50-A); registering the software.

## 5. What the website must change now
1. The events to capture, cookie-free and consent-based (a first-party event schema, stored without personal data in the repo).
2. Structured fields in the quiz and the Concierge (origin country, region, budget band, timing, financing) with the consent line for aggregated research.
3. A public "Dados" teaser (one aggregate chart a month) that shows sponsors and buyers what we know, if the operator approves.
4. What to stop doing or change on the site because of this.

## 6. Report
The memo, the table (product · buyer · price · comparable · effort · exit value · start when), and a 90-day sequence. Site changes in §5 proposed, not shipped, until the operator says go. One line on anything you could not verify.
