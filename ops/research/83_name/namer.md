# Prompt 83, agent 1: 80 candidate .com names built on "Maré"

Date: 12 Nov 2026. Research and writing only. No network request was made, so nothing here says whether a domain is free: that is the domain checker's job. Nothing was bought, registered or created. Notes on existing brands come from memory and are marked "unsure" where they need a check by the trademark agent.

## Base risks shared by all 80 names

These apply to every row and are not repeated in the table. "Base risks only" in the risk column means the name was checked in Portuguese, Brazilian Portuguese, French and English and nothing more was found.

1. English: "mare" is a female horse. Without the accent an English reader says "mair", and "mare" also sits inside "nightmare".
2. French: "la mare" is a pond. Said aloud, "Maré" is close to "marée" (tide, which helps) and to "marre" ("j'en ai marre", fed up).
3. Brazil: "Maré" is also the name of a favela complex in Rio, and the word appears in "maré de azar" (a run of bad luck).
4. The domain drops the accent, so every name must be spelled once on the phone ("mare, sem acento").

## What was ruled out, and why

Bad meanings found:
1. Seasick words: mareia, mareio, mareo, mareado, mareada, marear. In Portuguese and Spanish they mean to get seasick or dizzy.
2. Disaster or bad-luck words: maremoto (tsunami), maremorta (dead tide), marebaixa and marevazia (low tide, a slump), maredeazar (run of bad luck), marenegra (oil spill, as French "marée noire").
3. Rio favela readings: damare ("da Maré"), namare ("na Maré"), marerio and maredorio. In Brazil these read as the favela complex first. portugalnamare is kept in the list with this risk stated and a low rank.
4. Horse and pond readings made worse: themare ("the mare"), lamare (French "la mare", the pond), mareday and marenight (too close to "nightmare"), marais (sounds like "Maré" in French but means swamp).
5. Other: pegamare ("pega" has vulgar senses in European Portuguese), mareva (a "Mareva injunction" is an asset-freezing order in English law, a bad echo for a brand near property).

Names that are someone else's known brand:
1. Listed in the brief: maremagnum, marenostrum, maresia, and the exact name Amaré. "amare" stays in the list only because the operator's prompt names it as an example; it is flagged and ranked 47.
2. Found while working: mareterra (a new seafront district and property development in Monaco), maresvivas (a music festival in northern Portugal), marea (a car model, a Spanish rock band and a New York restaurant), mares (diving equipment), maresagres (a beer), vejamare ("Veja" is a Brazilian news magazine).
3. Kept but flagged for the trademark agent: marelusa and lusamare (national news agency), marelusofona (university), mareportugalia (airline and restaurant chain), mareviva (festival name in the plural), entremare, vivamare, marealta, mareazul, maremar, casamare, lamaree (all marked "unsure").

Over 14 characters: maredoatlantico (15), maresdeportugal (15), mareatlanticsul (15), mareportuguesas (15), marecorrespondencia (19).

Too close to Farol: marefarol, marefaro, marephare, marebeacon, marelighthouse, and the light words mareluz and marelume, which point back to a lighthouse.

Grammar: maredoportugal and mareportugues are wrong in Portuguese ("de Portugal", and "maré" is feminine), so they were left out.

## Open points for the other agents

1. Short and generic names (mareportugal, amare, maree, maresul, marealta, mareazul, getmare) are the most likely to be taken. The ranking ignores availability.
2. Every "unsure" in the table is a memory of an existing use, not a checked fact.
3. maremedia has a real weakness in Portuguese ("average tide"). It is ranked 10 for its use as a company name; the operator may weigh this differently.
