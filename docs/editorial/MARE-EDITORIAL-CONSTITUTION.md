# MARÉ — Editorial and production constitution
**Version 1.0 · 11 November 2026 · Applies to site, inbox, Maré articles, social and eventual annual print anthology.**

## First, approval
The owner chooses the story BEFORE research, writing, photography or design. Present 2–3 short pitches with the angle, source candidates and visual treatment. Only work on selected topics. **Approving the topic is not approving the finished issue, publishing or sending.** Human review of complete text, native languages, images, mobile/email rendering, subject/preheader and source checklist is required before distribution. Keep launch copies in draft/PR until approved.

## What Maré is
An actual Portugal-focused publication that happens to arrive by email: **Tuesday The Brief** (smart, surprising, ~3 minutes) and **Friday The Escape** (sensory, people and places, ~3–4 minutes), at 08:00 Lisbon time, first send date 1 December 2026 (and Friday 4 December). A shared editorial heart for **all three audiences**: people who love Portugal, diaspora/families, and potential property buyers/travelers. Never reduce a story to a property funnel; real estate is context when truly relevant. All three *languages* are written natively: pt-PT, en-US and fr; no untranslated locale is falsely presented as ready. The Brazil market may need locally adapted Portuguese separately.

**North star:** one surprising and true detail a reader will joyfully repeat at dinner; human narrative with tension, texture, originality, payoff and respect. Exceptional taste, no attitude. The standard is good enough to re-read in a beautiful printed annual after 52 weeks.

## Craft before format
1. Find the actual interesting story: subject, a surprising connection, what changed, why it mattered and why it still matters. No researched noun soup.
2. Verify facts and chronology against primary professional sources/interviews, reliable reporting, award institutions or archives. Cite precise URLs, checked dates. Distinguish quotes, interpretations and sourcing. Correct errors before sending; preserve post-release errata.
3. Open with a scene or unusually sharp fact that has a verifiable basis, not manufactured fiction or invented dialogues. Report on public figures' public professional activity only; no private addresses.
4. A good story does NOT require a property CTA, partner banner, generic disclaimer or forced seven-block structure. The publication is the product.
5. Tuesday: e.g., Pritzker laureates Siza and Souto de Moura, with actual built works and a striking mentor relationship. Friday: e.g., a chef's career, the dish that inspired them and an authentic destination, without invented food-tasting experiences.
6. If a newsletter's life depends entirely on a time-sensitive fact, include as-of date. For print re-check and revise with original date context.

## Distinctive visual language; NEVER the failed old green template
- Paper white / warm ivory base, charcoal editorial typography, a restrained rust/copper accent. Forest green is an optional small brand note, **never the whole backdrop**. Beautiful typography, non-grid layouts, deliberate editorial pace, no cartoon cards or PowerPoint.
- One strong relevant hero per issue. An optional diagram/infographic/pull quote can clarify an argument. **No generic stock photo as though it depicts a subject**; factual captions must distinguish contextual imagery.
- Image delivery in email: actual public HTTPS absolute `https://www.imoveisportugal.pt/...` URLs that return HTTP 200, `image/*` MIME and content without cookie or referrer. Restrict to suitable widths/KB, alt, width and height. Some readers block remote images by default: headline/story/quiz must still make sense. Send a real test to Gmail, iOS Mail and Outlook (where practical), not iframe alone.
- Photo/illustration rights ledger with author, source, explicit email/web and **print** rights, attribution, crop/change log. No scraping Michelin/restaurant photos to republish without permission. For CC-BY follow attribution; CC-BY-SA adaptations require share-alike compliance. Correct caption first.
- Aim for beautiful, enduring graphics that can later be typeset for the Annual; archive print-resolution originals and rights independently from downsized email images.

## Delight and gamification
Each edition contains ONE compact playful moment: an A/B/C quiz, which-building, spot-the-detail, city/photo reveal, small visual puzzle or genuine poll. **Answer appears immediately on a real mobile web page**; no waiting a week, fake game mechanic, excessive animation, sign-up gate or collection of salary/email in query strings. Buttons in email are normal links (email clients don't execute JS). On the linked answer page show correct response, one rewarding fact, original source, retry and return. Keyboard accessible, reduced-motion friendly, no fabricated vote percentages.

## Email vs web
- Email HTML: ~600 px center-aligned table, responsive, inline critical styles and fonts with fallbacks, clear preheader, short distinct subject, color and image-blocking safe. No embedded scripts, CSS-only gimmicks, background image dependence or downloadable random foreign images.
- Website: faithful storytelling, perhaps richer interactive graphics, clear link back and mobile QA. Never equate an iframe preview with a tested inbox.
- Archive: structured dates, story, source, authorship, edition/language, photography and licenses, quiz answer, usable for future collectible annual. The annual is not just emails printed and stapled.

## Operating states
`pitch proposed` → `subject approved` → `sources verified` → `draft` → `editorial accepted` → `visual/email tested` → `send approved` → `sent` → `archive and rights tracked`.
Only the owner authorizes the final two state transitions. Drafts in `docs/editorial/mare` are **NOT** sendable and should not be copied into `content/mare/issues` or a Beehiiv campaign automatically.
Older assets in `content/inbox` are historical/legacy marketing drafts, NOT Maré editorial templates. Never use outdated Thursday schedules or unsigned sponsors.
