# The master, the apprentice, and the rock.

*Maré · The Brief — Tuesday, December 1, 2026*

Just north of Porto, two celebrated buildings seem to have been designed by completely different forces.

At Leça da Palmeira, Álvaro Siza allowed the rocks and the Atlantic to do much of the talking. The Piscinas das Marés, designed in the 1960s, don't try to outshine the coast. Low concrete walls, salt water and the ocean's horizon make a public swimming pool feel like an unusually thoughtful conversation with the landscape.

In Braga, Eduardo Souto de Moura took a bolder approach: he built a soccer stadium into a former quarry. He didn't disguise the rock face. He made it part of the architecture.

One design recedes into the landscape. The other looks as if it has been carved out of it.

And here's the connection you may not know: **Souto de Moura spent five years working in Siza's studio.**

They would go on to win architecture's most prestigious international prize. Siza received the Pritzker in 1992. Souto de Moura followed in 2011, nineteen years later.

That makes for a lovely piece of architectural family history. But it also tells us something about Portugal: remarkable buildings don't always need grand entrances or flashy skylines. Sometimes the cleverest decision is what the architect chooses *not* to remove.

There is one more twist. Souto de Moura began his education studying sculpture before switching to architecture. His official Pritzker biography mentions meeting the American artist Donald Judd in Zurich as part of that change of direction. Once you know it, the Braga stadium is hard to see quite the same way.

You don't need to know the difference between brutalism and modernism to enjoy either place. Stand beside the pools at Leça. Look at the rock behind the stands in Braga. Two architects, two very different answers to the same question: what should a building do with the land it inherits?

And one final thought: a former student doesn't need to copy a master to prove how much he learned.

### Three small dates worth remembering

**1960s.** Siza designs the Piscinas das Marés.

**1992.** Siza receives the Pritzker Prize.

**2011.** Souto de Moura receives the Pritzker.

### Ten-second challenge

Who spent five years working in the other's studio?

A. Siza worked for Souto de Moura.

B. Souto de Moura worked for Siza.

C. They never worked together.

**Answer: B.** The Pritzker's official biography documents the relationship.

### Worth seeing

The tidal pools at Leça da Palmeira, then Braga Municipal Stadium. Two places where the ground itself is part of the story.
