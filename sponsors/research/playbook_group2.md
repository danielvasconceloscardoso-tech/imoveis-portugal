# Playbook research, group 2: premium media

Prompt 54 §1 (input to §2 playbook and §3 plan). Research date: 2 Nov 2026. Every fact carries its source and the source's publication date. "Secondary" means the figure was reported by someone other than the company or the person, or reached us only through a search-engine snippet because the page blocks automated fetching. Quotes are under 15 words. No podcast minute marks are given: no transcript with timestamps was reachable for the episodes cited (see "Could not verify").

## 1. Not Boring (Packy McCormick): the sponsored deep dive

**Model.** A free essay newsletter funded by sponsors instead of a paid tier. He chose this because free grows faster: "I could probably make a lot more money over time with sponsorships" (A Not Boring Adventure, One Year In, https://www.notboring.co/p/a-not-boring-adventure-one-year-in, 5 Apr 2021).

Ideas that matter for us:

1. **Two products only, priced by day and by depth.** A top-of-letter sponsorship ("150 words and a logo") and a sponsored deep dive, a whole Thursday issue on one company. "I charge a little more for Mondays than Thursdays" because Monday issues spread more; "I've grown rates in line with audience size ever since" (One Year In, 5 Apr 2021). Reported prices in March 2021, at about 40,000 subscribers: headline sponsorship $3,000–5,000 by day, deep dive $20,000; sponsorships bringing $30,000–40,000 a month by his own estimate (The Rebooting, https://therebooting.substack.com/p/ads-are-still-a-great-business-model, 17 Mar 2021; secondary).
2. **A written selection test, published to readers.** His "Sponsored Deep Dive Thought Process": he only writes about a company if he can answer yes to "Would I invest in this company if given the chance?" and would write about it unpaid; it must teach something beyond the company; he accepted "less than a third" of requests; he spends 40+ hours per dive and the company is involved in about 2 of every 20 hours; "I won't take money to do hit pieces"; "I don't want these to be 5,000 word ads" (Google Doc linked from Not Boring, https://docs.google.com/document/d/1K24dAK5k7LHuYMsJMyd2acDSqw8bEdJm_pciVrmVNXA, undated, written early 2021 since it cites the Jan–Mar 2021 dives).
3. **Use the sponsor as an excuse to cover a bigger subject.** The Masterworks dive became a piece on the art market; Ramp a piece on counter-positioning (same doc). Readers told him paid pieces must be "even more critical" than normal ones (One Year In, 5 Apr 2021).
4. **Sold by publishing the deck, not by outbound sales.** He surveyed readers (Aug 2020, at 10,642 subscribers: https://www.notboring.co/i/870527/verified-not-boring), built a deck from the survey plus past sponsors, and tweeted it; that thread filled the rest of 2020 (One Year In).
5. **Later evolution: an annual presenting sponsor and investor disclosure.** By 2025 issues open with "2025 Presenting Sponsor… Ramp" (https://www.notboring.co/p/meter-the-internet-utility, 30 Jan 2025). Recent deep dives disclose his own investment in the company ("I invested in Airbound's $37M … Series A", https://www.notboring.co/p/airbound-as-we-may-move, 28 Sep 2026).

**Versus the newsletter-native model.** Agrees on free plus sponsors; disagrees on format: one long, analytical, opinionated sponsored piece instead of several short native ads per issue.

## 2. Puck (Jon Kelly): premium, paid tiers, insider voice

Ideas that matter for us:

1. **Subscriptions first, but advertising became the larger line.** Puck launched in 2021 as a paid product; by 2023 advertising was "likely bigger than subscriptions" (The Rebooting, https://therebooting.substack.com/p/pucks-subs-ads-formula, 5 Sep 2023). Axios reported more than $20M revenue on track for 2025, mostly ads and sponsorship, nearly 50,000 paid subscribers, not profitable (Axios, https://www.axios.com/2025/10/30/puck-air-mail-stock-deal-graydon-carter, 30 Oct 2025; secondary, via search snippet, Axios blocks fetching).
2. **Scarcity is the ad product.** Kelly: "In a world of too much, scarcity matters." He rules out the scale programmatic needs and says Puck is as selective about advertisers as about stories; small, relationship-based sales teams are enough for "endemic" categories (Hollywood, Washington) (The Rebooting, 5 Sep 2023; companion podcast The Rebooting Show ep. 87, 5 Sep 2023, about 46 min).
3. **Tiers and access, not volume.** Member tier $100/year (puck.news price page, via search snippet; page returns 403 to direct fetch) and a $250 premium tier with networking events for those members; Q1 2023 events included a Sundance executive dinner and New York screenings (Digiday, https://digiday.com/media/how-newsletter-publishers-are-expanding-and-diversifying-beyond-inbox-based-revenue, 12 Apr 2023). Axios describes the model as sponsored roundtable dinners and events for high-end subscribers (Axios, 8 Aug 2023; secondary).
4. **The insider voice is the brand, and writers share the upside.** Writers get equity and a share of the subscription revenue they generate (TheWrap, https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/business/puck-redbird-investment-250-million-valuation/, 26 Aug 2026). The New Yorker called the tone "deliberately clubby" (2 Dec 2022, cited by Wikipedia; secondary).
5. **Exit path: buy adjacent premium titles, then sell control.** Puck bought Air Mail in a cash-and-stock deal valued at $16M (TheWrap, https://www.thewrap.com/puck-acquires-air-mail-graydon-carter-steps-down/, 30 Oct 2025). RedBird then took a majority stake at about $250M valuation; founders' memo: "This is about accelerating our strategy, not changing it" (TheWrap, https://thewrap.com/industry-news/deals-ma/redbird-capital-puck-majority-stake-250-million, 24 Sep 2026). The valuation was about $75M three years earlier (A Media Operator, https://www.amediaoperator.com/analysis/what-the-puck/, 28 Aug 2026). Warning: the Saks Global lawsuit over alleged undisclosed conflicts (Oct 2025, per Wikipedia; secondary) shows that insider access creates conflict risk.

**Versus the newsletter-native model.** Disagrees on scale and price: few readers, high price per reader, paid tiers, dinners; rejects programmatic.

## 3. The Real Deal (Amir Korangy): real-estate media, events, the luxury property advertiser

Ideas that matter for us:

1. **Independence is the founding pitch.** Korangy's 2010 publisher's note describes prior property coverage as "fluffy at best and incredibly influenced by advertisers at worst" and says developers regularly ask TRD to pull stories (https://therealdeal.com/magazine/new-york-march-2010/publisher-s-note-getting-it-right, Mar 2010; secondary, via search snippet, the site returns 403). His 20th-anniversary letter repeats "readers first" (https://therealdeal.com/magazine/national-april-2023/publishers-letter-20-years-of-putting-readers-first, Apr 2023; via search snippet).
2. **Events are editorial products.** The NYC Forum draws 3,000+ "active dealmakers", 100+ exhibitors; panels are moderated by TRD editors, "avoiding 'pay-to-play' puff pieces"; tickets in GA, VIP, subscriber discount, group rate and a Power Breakfast add-on (TRD event page, https://events.therealdeal.com/new-york/ai-info, updated Jan 2026). Exhibitor page lists a VIP Green Room sponsored by Bosch, Thermador and Gaggenau (https://advertising.therealdeal.com/events/nyc-exhibitors, 2025). Premium tier: Future City, an off-the-record three-day retreat at Baha Mar, Bahamas (media kit, Q4 2023).
3. **Rankings and a yearly data book sell print.** The annual Data Book carries "80+ rankings" (biggest brokerages, most active developers) and monthly issues each carry rankings (TRD media kit Q4 2023, https://static.therealdeal.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/4th-Quarter-TRD-Media-Kit-2023.pptx.pdf, Aug 2023). Precedent for our municipal index plus a "Portugal 100".
4. **Labelled native content plus a brand studio.** Sponsored content runs in the feed with "Sponsored content labeling (copy + logo)"; a lead-generation option is sold separately (same media kit). For us, D6 forbids per-lead pricing.
5. **Scale figures (self-reported, Q4 2023 kit):** 438K newsletter subscribers, 3.2M monthly visitors, 150K print readers, 10K+ event attendees a year; newsletter open rate 33–52%, CTR 4.5–10%, ads in newsletters 0.22% CTR; 100% share of voice for 24 h per newsletter slot.

No rate card, revenue or valuation is public. Korangy remains majority shareholder (TRD about page, via search snippet).

**Versus the newsletter-native model.** A trade title for professionals: print, rankings, data and large paid events alongside newsletters; ad CTR in the kit is low (0.22%) and the value is sold as presence and events, not clicks.

## 4. Wine Spectator and Cigar Aficionado (Marvin R. Shanken, 1943–2026)

Ideas that matter for us:

1. **Ratings are only worth something if advertisers cannot touch them.** 1983 editorial: the magazine is "not 'a promotional arm' for any wine, or any wine region" (quoted in obituary, https://www.winespectator.com/articles/marvin-r-shanken-visionary-publisher-who-transformed-wine-dies, 29 Oct 2026). He added a "not recommended" category and "advertisers were unhappy"; "we're not perceived to be the voice of the wine trade" (Shanken's own account, https://www.winespectator.com/articles/marvin-shanken-wine-spectator-history-50-anniversary-113026, 20 Oct 2026). Condé Nast's Jonathan Newhouse, 2019: "Marvin maintains an iron curtain" between ads and edit (obituary).
2. **Method published, blind, exceptions labelled.** Tasters know region, vintage and grape but not producer or price; non-blind scores are marked as such; taster initials show single-taster reviews (Wine Spectator "About Our Tastings", https://www.winespectator.com/articles/about-our-tastings, via search snippet). 100-point scale adopted in 1985 (obituary). Scores may not be altered or attached to other vintages (https://www.winespectator.com/articles/ratings-use, fetched 2 Nov 2026).
3. **Monetise the rating outside the editorial: licensing.** Shanken Ratings Network (Nov 2023): the only way for retailers to display the ratings; 425,000+ ratings in the database (https://www.shankennewsdaily.com/2023/11/03/34256/…, 3 Nov 2023). Brand owners pay an annual licence to use a rating or accolade in ads and packaging: $1,000 one brand, $2,500 up to three (https://www.winespectator.com/pages/shanken-ratings-network-for-brand-owners-and-suppliers, fetched 2 Nov 2026).
4. **Awards and events turn the rating into a community, and need guarding.** Restaurant Awards started 1981 with 13 Grand Awards; now 4,000+ restaurants hold an award (obituary). They are pay-to-enter: $595 early / $745 standard in 2025 (Wine Spectator application portal, via search snippet). In 2008 a critic won an Award of Excellence for a restaurant that did not exist after paying the then $250 fee; Wine Spectator called it a publicity-seeking scam (hoaxes.org, https://hoaxes.org/weblog/permalink/wine_spectator_hoaxed, Aug 2008; secondary). The Wine Experience: 4,000+ guests, two Grand Tastings of 250+ wines rated 90+, poured by owners; all net proceeds to the Scholarship Foundation, $40M+ raised (obituary). 2025 tickets about $420 per Grand Tasting night, about $2,695 weekend package (third-party listing, https://www.enprimeurclub.com/experiences/wine-spectator-new-york-wine-experience-2025; secondary). Big Smoke (Cigar Aficionado, since 1993) reportedly $394 to $1,295 (third-party; unverified).
5. **"We're in the education business."** Shanken: "We're not in the publishing business, we're in the education business" (obituary). He widened from wine to dining, travel and collecting in 1991 to reach "the mind, the heart and soul" of the reader. Circulation 5,000 (1979) → 76,000 (1988) → 385,000+ and about 1.5M readers per issue (obituary); the 50-year issue says the print run "approaches 375,000" (https://www.winespectator.com/articles/50-years-of-wine-spectator, 20 Oct 2026). He also built a paid trade data business (Impact Databank) and trade newsletters (Shanken News Daily).

**Versus the newsletter-native model.** The opposite: a long-horizon authority brand whose asset is a trusted score, monetised through print ads, licensing, awards and events. The rating is free to read, but its commercial use is paid.

## 5. Monocle and Winkreative (Tyler Brûlé): premium brand, retail, events, branded content

Ideas that matter for us:

1. **The ownership question: no 2025 sale is supported by the filings.** Winkontent Limited (UK operating company, no. 05265119) full accounts for the year to 31 Dec 2025, filed 26 Sep 2026, name the immediate parent Winkontent AG and the ultimate parent Winkorp AG (both Swiss), and give the ultimate controlling party as J T Brûlé (Companies House, https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/05265119/filing-history; read from the filed PDF). The PSC register lists him as the only person with significant control (fetched 2 Nov 2026). Earlier deals: Nikkei minority stake 2014 at about $115M valuation; Sansiri paid $6M for 12.5% in 2017, valuing it at $47M (Press Gazette, https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/thail-property-developer-pays-6m-for-minority-stake-in-monocle-ahead-of-asian-expansion-for-title/, 8 Nov 2017). No "Teo family" deal was found.
2. **Revenue comes from many small premium lines, and real estate and tourism boards are core advertisers.** FY2025 turnover £23.34M (FY2024 £21.12M), loss £3.13M (FY2024 £3.24M), going-concern material uncertainty, net liabilities funded by parent loans. Advertising +12%; print ads and advertorials +13% "driven by clients in fashion, design, inward investment, tourism promotion, and real estate"; special projects +13%; events +87% on ticket sales and sponsorship of the two main conferences; café and retail +22% "driven by an increasing number of brand-in-residence sponsorships"; newsstand and subscriptions −3% (same accounts, strategic report).
3. **Conferences as a high-price, small-room product, and Lisbon is home ground.** The first Quality of Life Conference was in Lisbon in 2015; the 2026 edition returned to Lisbon, 3–5 Sep, for about 200 delegates, at the Gulbenkian, gala at the Four Seasons Ritz, Christie's auctioneer running a charity auction (https://monocle.com/affairs/urbanism/qol/behind-the-scenes-quality-of-life-conference-lisbon-2026/, 18 Sep 2026). Ticket $2,600 (monocle.com shop, structured data, fetched 2 Nov 2026); 2025 Barcelona $2,400, $2,160 for subscribers, sold out (monocle.com shop, via search snippet).
4. **Branded content is the main ad format, with a light label.** Brûlé, 2017: "we do a lot of advertorials as well", the main growth area; Press Gazette found paid features flagged only with an "X", e.g. "Lisbon Tourism X Monocle" on a four-page Portugal feature, and called it a light touch on labelling rules (https://www.pressgazette.co.uk/tyler-brule-on-monocle-at-ten-and-how-it-is-making-more-money-from-print-journalism-than-ever-before/, 3 Mar 2017). Monocle was "largely reliant on sponsored content" for ad revenue (Press Gazette, 8 Nov 2017). The site now labels it "Partnered content" (monocle.com, Nov 2026). Our hard rule 4 requires a clearer label.
5. **Print and physical things as the luxury signal.** "People still want the tactile and the tangible" (Press Gazette, 3 Mar 2017). Then: 80,251 print circulation, £3M launch investment, said to be profitable since year four (same article; secondary for profitability). The 2025 accounts show the cost side: print, paper and logistics costs keep rising.

**Versus the newsletter-native model.** Disagrees almost entirely: print-first, retail and cafés, radio, expensive conferences, advertorial-led. The accounts show the cost of that breadth (recurring losses, parent funding).

## 6. Hodinkee (Ben Clymer): blog to commerce, brand partnerships, what went wrong

Ideas that matter for us:

1. **Editorial creates demand; the question is who captures it.** "We know what we do editorially sells things." He moved from 100% ads to 65% commerce / 35% ads in 2017; "We don't want to be the biggest anything", only "the most influential" (Digiday podcast, https://digiday.com/podcast/hodinkees-ben-clymer-flipping-from-advertising-to-commerce-revenue-is-difficult, 14 Aug 2018). Audience then: 90% men, average age 35–38, household income above $250,000 (same).
2. **Limited editions with brands are the commerce that works.** Since 2016 limited editions co-made with brands (a Vacheron Constantin edition sold out in about half an hour) (JCK, https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/hodinkee-stop-selling-watches/, 24 Jul 2024). These survived the 2024 retreat.
3. **What went wrong: buying inventory at the top of the market.** $40M Series B in Dec 2020 (LVMH Luxury Ventures, Chernin, Tom Brady, John Mayer), valuation over $100M; Crown & Caliber bought Feb 2021 for about $46M (WSJ, 22 May 2024, as quoted by JCK 24 Jul 2024 and by a 1 Jun 2024 repost; secondary). Then: revenue about $100M in 2021 fell to about $70M in 2023, five rounds of layoffs in 18 months, staff about 50 from a peak of about 160, unprofitable for several years (WSJ via former employees, 22 May 2024; secondary, company did not confirm revenue). In 2020, before the pivot, Clymer put revenue at about $25M from ads, events, insurance and a small shop (same WSJ piece; secondary).
4. **The reset: back to editorial.** July 2024 memo: no new pre-owned or new-model stock; "Suffice it to say, we've learned a lot"; "What we've done over the past few years simply wasn't that" (JCK, 24 Jul 2024; Adweek, https://www.adweek.com/media/hodinkee-winds-down-commerce/, 23 Jul 2024). Insurance with Chubb kept.
5. **Exit: sold to a retailer, editorial independence promised.** Watches of Switzerland Group acquired Hodinkee, price undisclosed; it "will run independently as a media organization"; Clymer returned as president (JCK, https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/watches-acquires-hodinkee/, 4 Oct 2024). A media brand owned by a retailer in its own category: the conflict our D9 rule avoids.

**Versus the newsletter-native model.** Proves audience trust can sell product, but also that owning inventory and scaling headcount destroyed value; the ad and editorial core survived.

## 7. Robb Report and Mansion Global (Dow Jones): how luxury property is sold in editorial

**Mansion Global (Dow Jones / Barron's Group).** Launched 1 May 2015 as a global luxury property news and listings site in English, Spanish and Chinese; listings at launch mainly from Sotheby's International Realty; launch sponsors Sotheby's International Realty, Extell and Sub-Zero/Wolf (Inman, https://www.inman.com/2015/05/01/news-corp-launches-luxury-global-real-estate-site-mansion-global/, 1 May 2015; via search snippet).

1. **Journalism is the sales argument; listings pay alongside.** Editor and publisher Mae Cheng: "people come to Mansion Global because of its journalism"; brokerages pay to put listings on the site; revenue also from display, events with clients and a separate custom-content arm; about 10 core staff and 50 original pieces a week; audience 40% US, 27% EMEA, 21% APAC; "In the luxury space, print is still very relevant" (Talking Biz News Q&A, https://talkingbiznews.com/highlighted-news/qa-mansion-global-is-a-growing-force-in-real-estate-coverage/, 1 Jul 2019).
2. **A public rate card for property advertisers (2025):** custom native article $25,000 net; custom newsletter $7,000 per send; 100% share of voice in the daily newsletter $3,680 per week; Saturday weekly newsletter $2,380; homepage hero listings $2,200–8,800/month; featured listing upgrade $1,089 per 90 days; new-development profile page with lead form $1,089 per 90 days (requires $2,000 campaign); "Experience Luxury" print insert to 100,000 WSJ subscribers, templated listing page $980, minimum listing price $1M; sponsored social posts tagged "paid partner" and subject to editorial approval (WSJ Residential Real Estate Overview 2025, https://res.cloudinary.com/remax-prod/images/v1757519026/us-global-resources-prod/WSJ-Residential-Real-Estate-Overview-_2025/WSJ-Residential-Real-Estate-Overview-_2025.pdf, 2025, hosted by a broker).
3. **Lesson for us:** the listings and lead-form products are per-property marketing for agencies, which D9 and D14 exclude for us. The transferable products are the flat newsletter takeover, the custom article and the print insert.

**Robb Report (Penske Media / Rockbridge).**

4. **Rate card benchmark for a European luxury title (Robb Report Germany 2026):** full page €22,200, outside back cover €34,600, spread €44,000; print run 55,000; 6 issues; cover price €14; a closing "Best of the Best" awards issue (Jahreszeiten Verlag media kit, https://jalag.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/ROBB-REPORT_Germany_MediaKit-Print_2026.pdf, Mar 2026).
5. **Ownership and events.** Rockbridge bought Robb Report in 2014 for a rumoured $60M; PMC formed a joint venture with Rockbridge at end 2016 to grow digital, subscriptions and events, terms undisclosed (Adweek, https://www.adweek.com/performance-marketing/robb-report-penske-media-corporation/, 31 Dec 2016). Robb Report Culinary Masters package was listed at $4,900 per couple in 2018 (third-party listing; dated, secondary). Its membership club (RR1, from the Robb Report Club founded 2010) has undisclosed dues (secondary).

## 8. Added: FT How To Spend It / HTSI

1. **Category caps on branded content.** Branded content was 30% of revenue; to avoid diluting editorial the FT worked with "one or two brands in any category" and never more than four or five branded campaigns at once; most luxury clients want 100% share of voice on the page (Digiday, https://digiday.com/media/inside-fts-lifestyle-supplements-rebrand/, 11 Oct 2016).
2. **Utility sells luxury.** 87% of readers had bought something found there; "People use it as a tool to book and buy" (same, FT luxury ad director). Small audience (190,000 monthly uniques) but core to luxury media plans.
3. **Tone matters to advertisers too.** Renamed HTSI in June 2022 to "reflect a world with deeper sensitivities" (editor Jo Ellison, FIPP, https://www.fipp.com/news/the-fts-how-to-spend-it-rebrands-as-htsi/, 1 Jun 2022). Relevant to our banned words ("paraíso", "oportunidade única").

## Where group 2 disagrees with the newsletter-native (Morning Brew) model

1. **Scale versus scarcity.** Puck, Monocle, TRD's Future City and the Wine Experience sell access to a small, rich room at high prices; Kelly rules out programmatic scale.
2. **Short native ads versus long sponsored pieces.** Not Boring and Monocle sell whole sponsored features; Mansion Global prices a custom article at $25,000 against $3,680 for a week of newsletter takeover.
3. **Click metrics versus authority.** TRD's own kit shows 0.22% ad CTR; Shanken, Monocle and Mansion Global sell trust, print presence, rankings and events, not clicks.
4. **Revenue beyond ads.** Ratings licensing (Shanken), retail and cafés (Monocle), commerce and limited editions (Hodinkee), listings (Mansion Global), paid tiers (Puck). Hodinkee shows the risk when that line requires stock and staff.
5. **Print.** Monocle, Wine Spectator, TRD, Mansion Global and Robb Report all keep print as the luxury signal; no newsletter-native brand in this group does.

## Lessons for a premium Portugal letter

1. **The index is the asset; keep it unpaid.** Copy Wine Spectator: published method, official data only, a "not recommended" or low band that is actually used, method exceptions labelled, no sponsor ever sees a score before publication. This is D69 as written.
2. **Do not charge places or developers to enter a ranking or award.** The Wine Spectator Restaurant Awards fee ($250 then, $745 now) produced the 2008 fake-restaurant hoax. D69's paid "dated badge for developments" carries the same risk; it needs a site check or document check and published refusals. Business decision for the CEO.
3. **Charge for commercial use of a result, not for the result.** Shanken licenses the score to the rated brand ($1,000/brand/year). A later product could license use of a published index score or Portugal 100 mention, flat fee, never affecting the score. This needs a CEO and legal decision before it is offered, because municipalities and developers are both subjects and buyers.
4. **One annual moment.** The Wine Experience, Robb Report's Best of the Best and TRD's Data Book show the value of a yearly reveal plus an event. Our Portugal 100 (late Nov reveal, #1 on 1 Dec) is that moment; sell one presenting sponsor for it, not a list of logos.
5. **Events: small, expensive, editor-hosted.** Monocle sold about 200 seats at $2,600 in Lisbon in 2026; Puck runs dinners for its $250 tier; TRD runs an off-the-record retreat. A Douro dinner or Lisbon walk for 20–40 readers, moderated by us, with one sponsor, fits. Panels are never pay-to-play (TRD rule).
6. **The sponsored deep dive as our top unit.** Packy's rules map directly: write only about sponsors we would cover anyway, accept a minority of requests, more critical than usual, cover the wider subject (e.g. a bank → how non-residents get mortgages), disclose any conflict. Price it far above a block (Not Boring 2021 ratio was about 4–7x a headline slot; Mansion Global's custom article is about 7x a week of newsletter takeover).
7. **Category caps and an obvious label.** Follow HTSI (max 1–2 brands per category, max 4–5 live campaigns), not Monocle's "X" or "Partnered content". Our label stays explicit (hard rule 4), and one sponsor per collection (D69).
8. **The real advertisers of premium property media are inward investment, tourism and real estate.** Monocle's 2025 accounts name them as growth drivers; Lisbon Tourism already bought a Monocle feature in 2017. Portuguese tourism boards, AICEP-type bodies and municipalities belong in the §4 sponsor list (procurement later), within D9.
9. **No inventory, no listings, no lead fees.** Hodinkee's damage came from owning stock and scaling staff; Mansion Global's and TRD's listings and lead forms are agency products that D6, D9 and D14 exclude. Our commerce stays flat sponsorships, paid guides and calls.
10. **Plan the exit around scarcity and a premium audience.** Puck went from about $75M to $250M by staying small and premium and buying an adjacent title; Monocle's minority stakes valued it at $47–115M while still loss-making; Hodinkee sold to a retailer in its own field. Buyers pay for a defined rich audience and an independent voice, so independence rules must be documented from day one.
