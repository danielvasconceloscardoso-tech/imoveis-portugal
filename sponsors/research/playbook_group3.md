## 1. Dan Oshinsky (Inbox Collective)

Former head of newsletters at BuzzFeed and The New Yorker. Has run the Inbox Collective consultancy since 2019 (P, https://inboxcollective.com/about-us/, n.d.).

1. **Charge on opens, not on list size.** He tells sellers to price on the number of people who actually open. His method: list size × average open rate ÷ 1,000 × CPM. His range is roughly **$15 to $50 per 1,000 opens**. One Inbox Collective page gives $15–50 and another gives $20–50. (P, https://inboxcollective.com/five-types-of-indie-newsletter-business-models/, 16 Sep 2022, date from a bookmark listing; P, https://inboxcollective.com/the-rise-of-the-100k-newsletter-operator/, n.d.; P, https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-monetize-your-newsletter-with-ads/, n.d.)
   - *For us:* our price list is per subscriber. If sponsors who know this method compare us on opens, our CPM roughly doubles (see the last section).
2. **The native ad is the anchor unit and smaller units hang off it.** Classifieds, job listings and event listings are 25–150 words of plain text. He prices them at about a quarter of the native ad: "usually somewhere like a quarter of whatever I'm charging for a big native ad" (P, https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-monetize-your-newsletter-with-ads/, n.d.).
   - *For us:* if the partner block is 250 €, a short Friday item would be about 60–100 € by his rule. We list the Friday weekend item at 400 €. That works only if the weekend item is a feature-length native unit and not a listing.
3. **Native ads must be clearly labelled because they blend in.** He defines them as written "in the voice and tone of the newsletter" (P, same URL). This matches our hard rule 4 and D6.
4. **A floor price for a 10,000-subscriber list.** An extract of his piece on selling mistakes says a 10k newsletter should charge "at least $500" for a native ad when it starts selling. The search could not show this sentence in context, so it is listed under **Could not verify** (https://inboxcollective.com/7-mistakes-people-make-when-selling-newsletter-ads/, n.d.).
5. **Readers unsubscribe much faster from dedicated sponsor emails than from the normal letter.** His advice is clear labelling and a preference centre (P, Inbox Collective owned-growth and ads pages, n.d., search extract). For us, this argues against selling dedicated sends early.

Related podcast: Nathan Barry, ep. 019, "Turn Your Newsletter Into a Business (Lessons from BuzzFeed)", https://nathanbarry.com/019-dan-oshinsky-turn-newsletter-business-lessons-buzzfeed/ (n.d., no minute marks).

## 2. Matt McGarry (Newsletter Operator, GrowLetter)

Runs GrowLetter, a growth agency. Named clients include The Daily Upside, Milk Road, The Hustle and Sahil Bloom (S, Passionfroot episode page below).

1. **Meta cost per subscriber has a published grading scale.** His benchmark post grades Meta CPA: "$2.00 = world class", $3 great, $4 good, $7 very bad. He adds that some consumer newsletters accept $3–5 (P, https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/newsletter-benchmarks, n.d., search index suggests about 2025).
2. **His own results sat above that benchmark.** He reports $23,398 spent for 6,060 subscribers, a **$3.86 CPA**, and an earlier Meta test at $5.89 for 100 subscribers. Twitter ads came in at $2.69 (P, https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/how-i-grew-to-10k-subscribers and related Newsletter Operator posts, n.d.).
3. **Paid channels ranked.** Meta $1–3 CPA if done well, with payback in 1–4 months. SparkLoop partner subscribers cost $1–2 CPA but convert at only 1–2% (P, https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/the-best-paid-acquisition-channels and https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/paid-acquisition-crash-course, n.d.).
4. **Spend in line with lifetime value.** His rule of thumb: with a $10 LTV per subscriber, paying $2–3 per subscriber from ads is comfortable (S, interview summarised at https://marketingagainstthegrain.com/articles/how-to-use-meta-ads-for-your-next-paid-newsletter-campaign, n.d.). In a talk he said under $2 for consumer (B2C) lists and under $4 for business (B2B) lists (S, https://financialmarketingsummit.com/financial-publishing/matt-mcgarry-inside-the-other-newsletter-business-boom/, n.d.).
   - *For us:* our audience is people abroad who own property and have high incomes. That should push CPA towards the B2B end, about $4, not $1–2. **No niche-specific CPA was found.**
5. **Beehiiv Boosts cost has fallen.** In his write-up of beehiiv's 2024 report, the average Boosts cost per subscriber was **$2.37 in 2023**, and he predicted it would fall (P, https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/takeaways-predictions-beehiivs-2024-state-newsletters-report, 2024).

Podcast: Passionfroot "Creators on Air", "The Secrets Behind the Most Successful Newsletters with Matt McGarry", about 36 min, listed 20 Sep 2023. Its chapters cover "when and how to start paid acquisition": https://passionfroot.me/creators-on-air/the-secrets-behind-the-most-successful-newsletters-with-matt-mcgarry (no minute marks; could not play).

## 3. Louis Nicholls (SparkLoop: paid recommendations, Upscribe, Partner Network)

Co-founder of SparkLoop, which ConvertKit (now Kit) acquired in 2023 (P, https://sparkloop.app/blog/sparkloop-joins-the-convertkit-family-focuses-on-paid-recommendations, 2023).

1. **Paid recommendations pay typically $2–7 per confirmed subscriber.** SparkLoop's help centre says Partner Network commissions "typically range from $2–$7 per confirmed referral", depending on the newsletter. The marketing page says $2–20 (P, https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/9184574-the-ultimate-guide-to-sparkloop-features-pricing, n.d.; P, https://sparkloop.app/partner-network, n.d.).
   - *For us:* this is both a **cost** (if we buy subscribers) and a **revenue line** (if we recommend other letters on our sign-up page).
2. **"Confirmed" has strict rules.** A referral must pass fraud checks and stay subscribed through a screening period of about 10–14 days. It must come from a country the buyer accepts. If the buyer's budget runs out, the referral is unpaid. SparkLoop keeps **20% plus about 3.5% in fees** (P, https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/9768833-everything-you-need-to-know-about-paid-recs and https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/7118861-understanding-paid-recommendation-budgets, n.d.).
3. **Money from the sign-up page on day one.** Nicholls put recommendation earnings at about $0.50–2.50 per new subscriber in a guest post (S/P, https://www.revenews.co/p/day-0-monetization, n.d.). In the Kit announcement he estimated paid recommendations could add about $2–5 per subscriber for creators (P, Kit/SparkLoop announcement above, 2023). On LinkedIn he cited Stacked Marketer earning about $2–3 per website sign-up (P, https://www.linkedin.com/posts/louisnicholls_after-a-short-hiatus-the-sparkloop-friday-activity-7136628179552641024-LBEf, about Dec 2023).
4. **Value per subscriber varies by model.** On Nathan Barry's podcast he valued his own list at about **$56 per subscriber** and said the average hides wide variation. He said newsletters selling paid courses show the highest LTV (P, https://nathanbarry.com/025-louis-nicholls-explode-list-growth-referral-program/, n.d., no minute marks).
5. **Who Sponsors Stuff on his podcast.** In Send & Grow S01E17 he interviews the Who Sponsors Stuff founders on prospecting, pricing and what to do after a weak campaign (P, https://sparkloop.app/blog/s01e17-how-to-sell-more-newsletter-ads-sponsorships-in-2023-and-beyond-with-ryan-sager-jesse-watkins-from-who-sponsors-stuff, 2023, 29 min; no minute marks).

## 4a. Paved (newsletter ad marketplace)

1. **CPM ladder by audience type:** **$3–30** for broad consumer, **$30–75** for niche audiences, **$75+** for premium and executive audiences (P, https://www.paved.com/blog/how-to-run-newsletter-sponsorships/, 2026 per title). Paved's CPM explainer calls **$15–30** "healthy" (P, https://www.paved.com/blog/cpm-for-email-marketing/, n.d.).
   - *For us:* a letter for people abroad buying property in Portugal can argue for "niche" pricing and, with proof of income or wealth, for "premium".
2. **Judge sponsorships by clicks, not only CPM.** In a study of Paved's most re-booked sponsorships, the average CPC was about **$5.00**. Most email sponsorships fall between **$1 and $5 per click** (P, https://www.paved.com/help/articles/10291875-a-publisher-s-guide-to-newsletter-sponsorship-pricing and https://www.paved.com/help/articles/8120741-how-much-do-newsletter-ads-cost, n.d.).
   - *For us:* report clicks to sponsors from the first letter, as /sponsor-report plans.
3. **The median listing is cheap.** A 2026 Paved guide reportedly gives an average CPM of $6.53 for general-interest newsletters and $23.01 for premium niches "for the most rebooked newsletters". A second search could not find these numbers, so they are under **Could not verify** (https://www.paved.com/blog/the-ultimate-newsletter-sponsorship-guide/).
4. **Paved's figures by category reach us only through other sites.** Outrank, citing Paved's Q3 2026 publisher benchmarks via PPC Land, gives about **$7.11 per 1,000 subscribers for finance and $3.94 for travel** per send. That is per subscriber, not per open (S, https://www.outrank.so/blog/blog-advertising-rates, 2026). VoxBooster attributes **about $85 CPM for finance and crypto** to Paved (S, https://voxbooster.com/blog/newsletter-sponsorship-statistics-2026/, 2026; its tables are garbled). The two cannot both be right on the same basis. Treat both as unconfirmed.
5. **Fees.** A third-party site says Paved takes 30% of marketplace bookings and 40% of ad-network earnings (S, https://sponsorships.ai/alternatives/paved, n.d.; not confirmed on Paved's site). Selling direct keeps the full price.

## 4b. Passionfroot (creator sponsorship platform)

1. **Classifieds are the cheapest unit.** "they are usually the cheapest category of advertisement" (P, Passionfroot Sponsorship Pricing Guide PDF, hosted at https://files.tryflowdrive.com/293/file-d1af3435-221b-4da4-b1de-4e3aa34a81b6_Passionfroot-Pricing-Guide.pdf, n.d.; third-party host, so the version is not confirmed).
2. **Its rate of thumb is $15–30 CPM** for an average newsletter. Its creator spotlight (Houck's Newsletter) cites a $12.5 CPM and a $0.37 CPC as "very low". It compares this with LinkedIn at about $20–30 CPM (same PDF).
   - *For us:* sponsors compare a newsletter slot with LinkedIn and Meta CPMs, so the media kit should show cost per click and per reader next to those.
3. **Platform data is kept private.** Passionfroot has an internal dataset of B2B creator prices ("Creator Graph") but publishes no rate report (S, https://www.netinfluencer.com/b2b-creator-marketing-platform-passionfroot-raises-15m-usd-series-a-led-by-insight-partners/, 2025).
4. **Fees** are reported as 5% on direct bookings and 15% on Partner Network deals. A paid tier from April 2025 is also reported (S, https://creatoreconomytools.com/tool/passionfroot and https://mediapact.ai/compare/passionfroot/, 2026; not confirmed on Passionfroot's own pages).

## 4c. Who Sponsors Stuff (what sponsors buy)

1. **It is a sponsor database, not a price index.** It tracks which brands advertise in which newsletters. Its own pages cite **250+ newsletters and 4,000+ sponsors** in one place and **500+ and 10,000+** in another (P, https://signup.whosponsorsstuff.com/, n.d.). It is run by Email Revenue Solutions, which sells ads for publishers.
2. **Its own pricing:** a sponsored listing costs **$50 a month**, and a listing plus a newsletter ad costs **$100 a month** (P, https://signup.whosponsorsstuff.com/sponsored-listing, n.d.).
   - *For us:* this is useful for prospecting under §4. Search it for banks, FX firms and airlines that already buy newsletter ads.
3. **Selling method:** its founders discuss prospecting from who already sponsors similar newsletters, pricing, and saving a weak campaign. Send & Grow, 2023 (S01E17, link in section 3) and 2024 ("Navigating Newsletter Ads in 2024", https://sendandgrow.transistor.fm/episodes/ryan-sager-jesse-watkins-of-who-sponsors-stuff); no minute marks.
4. **No CPM or rate-by-category report** from Who Sponsors Stuff was found. A Book a Sponsor study (a different company) is in the table (S, Oct 2026).

## 5. beehiiv, Substack, Kit, Sponsy, Swapstack, Letterhead

1. **beehiiv open and click rates.** The 2026 State of Newsletters page reports a **41% average open rate in 2025**: "The average email open rate in 2025 was 41%." (P, https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/the-state-of-newsletters-2026, early 2026, exact date n.d.). Secondary coverage gives 41.24% (up from 37.98% in 2024) and a click rate of **3.23%, down from 4.74%** (S, https://ppc.land/newsletter-monetization-shifts-toward-sponsorships-as-paid-models-plateau/, 2026).
2. **beehiiv ad network.** It pays by unique open on CPM; the advertiser sets the rate. beehiiv publishes **no average CPM** (P, https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/17525818225431-using-the-beehiiv-ad-network, n.d.). The 2025 report says the network paid **$3,726,453.84** to newsletters, and 4,728 newsletters ran network ads in 2024 (P, https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/2025-state-of-email-newsletters-by-beehiiv, early 2025). Variety reports more than **$1M a month** in payouts in 2026 (S, https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/beehiiv-advertising-network-creators-newsletters-1236626247/, 2026).
   - *Rough average, my arithmetic:* $3.73M ÷ 4,728 ≈ **$790 per newsletter for the whole year** (D). Self-serve network ads earn little. Direct sales are where the money is.
3. **beehiiv Boosts (paid recommendations).** beehiiv's own page shows a **$1.63 average cost per subscriber** and a 42% open rate for boosted subscribers, footnoted as "preliminary results from those with early access" (P, https://www.beehiiv.com/boosts-promo, n.d.). The 2023 average was $2.37 (via McGarry, above). beehiiv's free plan does not include the ad network or Boosts as a seller. This affects us only once we upgrade (CLAUDE.md stack note; not re-checked here).
4. **Paid newsletters, for contrast.** beehiiv reports median paid-subscriber revenue of **$83–230 per subscriber**, depending on vertical (P, https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/the-state-of-paid-newsletters-2026, 2026, search extract). That is subscription money, not ads. It shows what a paid tier could be worth later.
5. **Kit (ConvertKit).** Its State of the Creator Economy reports (2022, 2023, 2024) give creator income bands but no sponsorship CPMs in the extracts I saw (P, https://convertkit.com/reports/creator-economy-2024). Kit now owns SparkLoop. **Substack, Sponsy:** no published sponsorship price data found. **Swapstack** explains pricing per 1,000 opens versus flat rate but showed no averages (P, https://www.swapstack.co/newsletter-pricing-101, n.d.). **Letterhead** uses the worked example "$30 CPM × 10,000 subscribers = $300 per sponsor" (P, https://blog.tryletterhead.com/blog/newsletter-ad-pricing-tips-tricks-maximize-revenue, n.d.).

## What this says about our draft prices

Assumptions for the arithmetic: Tuesday and Friday ≈ **8.7 letters a month, 104 a year**; open rate **45%**. That sits above beehiiv's 41%, which is reasonable for a premium niche, but it is still an assumption. **"Per 1,000 opens"** means the price divided by the number of readers who open, in thousands. Euro and dollar are treated as roughly equal (see method note).

| Subscribers | Block 250 € per letter: € per 1,000 subscribers / per 1,000 opens | Above 10k: 60 € per 1,000 subscribers |
|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 250 / ~556 | n/a |
| 5,000 | 50 / ~111 | n/a |
| 10,000 | 25 / ~56 | (600 € if the 60 € rule applied) |
| 50,000 | n/a | 3,000 € per letter = 60 / ~133 per 1,000 opens |

1. **At 5k–10k subscribers the 250 € block is market price.** It equals about 56–111 € per 1,000 opens. That is inside Paved's "niche" band ($30–75) and above it at 5k. Book a Sponsor's median main slot for 5k–20k lists is $300. If the Oshinsky "at least $500 at 10k" floor is confirmed, the block is underpriced at 10k.
2. **At 1k subscribers, no CPM benchmark supports 250 €.** That is about 556 € per 1,000 opens, roughly 7–10× the top of every range found. A sponsor buys it only as a **Founding Partner** brand association with a locked price, not for reach. The media kit should say so plainly and not show a CPM until about 5k.
3. **The 60 € per 1,000 subscribers rule above 10k is at the premium end.** At 45% opens it is about 133 € per 1,000 opens. That fits Paved's "premium/executive $75+" band only if we can show audience wealth (owner or buyer intent, income). Otherwise sponsors will compare it with the $15–50 general range. It also creates a **price jump at 10,000**: the block goes from 250 € to 600 € per letter, 2.4× overnight. A smooth curve avoids that jump, for example 250 € up to 5k, then 40–45 € per 1,000 up to 20k, then 60 €.
4. **Monthly bundles are a fair discount.** The 1,500 € block per month is about 173 € per letter, 31% off single letters. The six-letter series at 2,500 € is about 417 € per letter, so it is priced as a premium product, not a bulk buy. The series therefore needs a visible extra (editorial theme, title mention) to justify paying more than a block. Note: Oshinsky prices short units at ¼ of the native ad. On that rule the **400 € Friday weekend item** is high next to a 250 € block, unless it is a full native feature.
5. **"Apresentado por" at 4,000 € a month (~460 € per letter, ~48k € a year) has no peer in this research below 50k subscribers.** At 10k it is about 102 € per 1,000 opens. That is plausible for one exclusive bank or developer. But it is 4–6× the Gold package in CLAUDE.md (8–12k € per year). That inconsistency is a **CEO decision**, not a CTO one.
6. **Ceiling revenue per subscriber.** If one block sells on every letter: 26k € a year. Add title sponsorship and the series: ~70–80k € a year. At 10k subscribers that is **2.6–8 € per subscriber per year**, in line with Morning Brew (~$7), The Hustle (~$6.7) and 1440 (~$5.4), all derived. At 1k subscribers the same revenue would be 26–80 € per subscriber, which only founding-partner deals can deliver. Do not plan cash flow on full sell-through.
7. **Growth cost and price must fit together.** Paid acquisition runs about $1.6–4 per subscriber: Boosts $1.63, Meta $2–4, SparkLoop $2–7. Getting to 10k by paid means alone would cost about **16–40k €** (D). That is roughly what a year of blocks at 10k brings in, so paid growth pays back only if prices hold above 5k. Selling recommendations on our own sign-up page (about $0.50–2.50 per new subscriber) is a small income line that fits the "flat, never per-lead" rule only if the operator agrees that recommendation fees are not lead fees. **Flag for CEO** (D6).
8. **Exit context.** Brand-scale newsletter deals reported about **3–5× revenue**: Axios ~5×, Morning Brew ~3.75–5.8× (D), The Hustle ~2.7× (est.), 1440 ~4× (valuation only). Small newsletters on marketplaces trade at **about 2–3.5× annual profit**. Price discipline and documented sell-through raise the multiple more than list price does.
