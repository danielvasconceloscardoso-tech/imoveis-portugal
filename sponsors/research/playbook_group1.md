## 1. Morning Brew: Alex Lieberman, Austin Rief

**Idea 1. Write the ad in the letter's voice, and staff that job.** Sponsors get native placements in each issue, "written by a Morning Brew copywriter and approved by the client". The team went from one copywriter in August 2019 to four in 2020, and was hiring a fifth.
- Digiday, 7 Feb 2019 (secondary, citing Rief and Lieberman): https://digiday.com/media/morning-brew-claims-1-million-subscribers-3m-revenue-profitable-business
- Digiday, 5 Aug 2020 (secondary, with Lieberman quotes): https://digiday.com/media/let-the-buyers-know-you-exist-how-morning-brew-plans-to-grow-brand-ad-dollars-from-its-base-of-direct-response
- What it looks like today (primary, my own reading of the issue dated 2 Nov 2026, retrieved 2026-11-02): one "Presented By" slot at the top, three "Sponsored By" native blocks of about 50 to 130 words each in the house voice (Incogni, Justworks, Johnsonville), two one-line classified links marked "*A message from our sponsor", and a separate affiliate disclosure. That is about six paid elements in one daily issue. Note the Johnsonville headline, "Meat the candidate for dinner 2026", which ties the ad to the day's news the way an editorial joke would. https://www.morningbrew.com/issues/latest

**Idea 2. Price on engagement, not on list size.** Rief, 2019: "For the most part, [we sell on] a CPV. It's cost per thousand opens." A logo at the top plus a sponsorship in the middle "goes for somewhere between $60 and $70 CPV". His reason: "If we grow in sketchy ways and people aren't opening, you won't pay for those."
- Digiday Podcast write-up, 9 May 2019 (primary quotes): https://digiday.com/media/morning-brew-austin-rief-media-email-newsletter
- Rief in 2025 on why engagement still sets the price: if the content is weak, at 500k to 1M subscribers "their ads don't sell for $50,000, they sell for $3,000". His summary: "It's all about the content." MFM-2025 [55:28 to 55:45].

**Idea 3. Sell out the calendar early, renew, then trade up from direct response to brand.** Early 2019: about $200,000 a week in ad revenue, with "90 percent of its ad inventory" for the first half of 2019 already sold (Digiday, 7 Feb 2019, citing a spokesperson). In 2019, 90% of revenue came from direct-response advertisers. The renewal rate was above 70%, and the goal was 50% brand revenue by the end of 2021 (Digiday, 5 Aug 2020). Rief on the two worlds: early on "we sold all direct response ads", and the pitch was 300 clicks with a 3% conversion, "we'll charge you $800". Then New York agencies opened "$5 million from the biggest brands", sold on relationships, not logic. MFM-2022 [18:35 to 19:31]. Discover was an early advertiser that "gave us a million bucks" while Rief was still in college. MFM-2022 [20:09]. Being near the agencies mattered: "you have to be where the ad agencies are." MFM-2025 [01:00:12].

**Idea 4. Grow with referrals and cross-promotion first; pay for subscribers only once retention is proven.** Referral tiers: 3 referrals unlock the Sunday "Light Roast" edition, which cannot be bought; 5 unlock stickers; 10 unlock the Facebook group; higher tiers unlock a mug (Rief, Digiday, 9 May 2019). More than 50,000 subscribers had referred at least three people. In 2018 they ran cross-promotions with CB Insights and NextDraft (Digiday, 7 Feb 2019). Paid acquisition came only after organic growth to 100k: "So 2015 and 2016 to get to 100,000 subscribers. It was entirely organic." Then they spent hard: about $500k a month on ads by the end of 2018, and $6 to 7M on paid acquisition in 2019. MFM-2025 [27:07 to 27:21], [30:01], [32:11].

**Idea 5. Go narrow for higher CPMs, and sell to a buyer who values stability.** On the B2B verticals: "The CPMs are higher. You don't need to grow as fast." MFM-2025 [32:37 to 32:54]. Lieberman: "the more niche, the better." MFM-2025 [55:59]. New B2B newsletters could "break-even before we even hired the writers" because an advertiser was pre-sold. MFM-2022 [09:01]. On why they sold, Rief: "ads businesses are quite volatile. They're not recession proof at all." Talking Biz News, 18 Mar 2021, excerpting Business of Business: https://talkingbiznews.com/they-talk-biz-news/why-morning-brew-sold-to-insider-and-axel-springer/ . In COVID, a $75k financial-services sponsorship was cancelled the day before it ran, and about 30% of booked revenue vanished within weeks. MFM-2025 [43:17 to 43:41].

**The sale.** Insider Inc. (Axel Springer) bought a majority stake in an all-cash deal in October 2020. The founders confirm "75 million I think is the public number". MFM-2025 [01:15]. Flashes & Flames (secondary, 16 Oct 2020) reported the deal at more than $75m, "almost 4 x $20m revenue expected in 2020": https://flashesandflames.com/2020/10/16/axel-springer-to-buy-morning-brew/ . The process ran about 11 months end to end. They pitched strategic buyers (SoFi) without success and took a media buyer partly out of fear of losing the only offer. MFM-2025 [46:04], [51:03 to 53:46]. Axel Springer took full ownership in February 2025 on undisclosed terms. Morning Brew was "on track to surpass $70 million in annual revenue in 2025" (Talking Biz News, 12 Feb 2025, secondary): https://talkingbiznews.com/media-news/axel-springer-buys-remaining-stake-in-morning-brew/

## 2. Axios: Jim VandeHei, Mike Allen, Roy Schwartz

**Idea 1. One native ad format only, clearly labelled, with no banners, pop-ups or programmatic.** Schwartz called "short-form native" "the only type of advertising that Axios uses", labelled as a "message from" the brand. Two of his lines: "There are no pop-ups, no banners, no X buttons to look for." "Programmatic is not something that's interesting to me." The newsletters use the same unit, and "a brand can tell you a sequential story over the course of a week".
- AdExchanger, 7 Mar 2018 (primary Q&A with Schwartz): https://adexchanger.com/the-sell-sider/axios-people-no-time-read-news-engage-ads/
- In early 2017, Digiday measured the longer of the two ads in Mike Allen's newsletter at "barely 175 words". Schwartz: "The big problem with native advertising is you have to get a click. We don't have to do that." Digiday, 10 Jan 2017 (primary Q&A): https://digiday.com/media/axioss-jim-vandehei-content/

**Idea 2. Sell time-boxed exclusivity, and charge more in the weeks that matter.** Axios newsletter sponsorships were "sold as one-week exclusives to each advertiser that buys one". During a high-interest week such as Election Day, the sales team "would have looked to sell that week's sponsorships at premium prices". Digiday, 25 Jan 2017 (secondary): https://digiday.com/media/newsletters-hot-monetization-strategies-bloom . This is the closest model to a flat "Apresentado por" week, and it gives a reason for seasonal premiums (golf weeks, Budget week).

**Idea 3. Brevity is the product, and it extends to ads.** VandeHei: "Ninety percent of stories either shouldn't have been written" or should be 10% the length. He rejects chasing scale: "you've made the deal with the devil." Digiday Podcast write-up, 5 Apr 2017: https://digiday.com/media/axios-jim-vandehei-digiday-podcast-jigs-publishers-worshipping-scale/ . The Smart Brevity method (Axios HQ's own primer, undated PDF): audience first; a headline "under 60 characters"; say "What's new" and "Why it matters" first; write like a human; stay scannable; "Stop when enough is enough". https://www.axioshq.com/hubfs/smart-brevity-101.pdf . Book: *Smart Brevity*, VandeHei, Allen and Schwartz, Workman, 2022.

**Idea 4. Premium paid tier for professionals; the ambition was larger than the result.** Axios Pro launched in January 2022 at $599 a year for one newsletter or $2,499 for all-access. It made about $2M in 2022 from more than 3,000 paid subscribers, while total revenue was "just south of $100 million". Schwartz's stated goal was for Pro to reach 50% of revenue. Pro was still "considered to be an investment", which Digiday's editor glossed as unprofitable. Digiday, 20 Jan 2023 (secondary, company figures): https://digiday.com/media/axios-pro-generated-2-million-in-2022-with-more-than-3k-paid-subscribers/ . VandeHei in 2017: "I'm not talking small dollar subscription" (Digiday, 5 Apr 2017).

**Idea 5. Profitable, brand-funded newsletters sold at about 5x revenue.** Cox Enterprises agreed to buy Axios for $525M in cash. Axios said it expected about $100M of revenue in 2022, and Cox added $25M of investment. Built In, 9 Aug 2022 (secondary): https://builtin.com/articles/axios-media-acquired-by-cox-enterprises-525m-080922 . Flashes & Flames (secondary, 11 Aug 2022) estimated revenue at $12m (2017), $25m (2018), $40m (2019), $60m (2020), $88m (2021) and $110m (2022, estimate). It reported Axios as EBITDA-positive since 2018, with $57m raised in total, most revenue from "brand advertising in its 19 national newsletters", and the price at about 5x 2022 revenue, like Politico and Industry Dive: https://flashesandflames.com/2022/08/11/axios-sale-follows-price-trend-5x-revenue/ . The $525M figure was first reported by the New York Times; Cox did not disclose it.

## 3. The Hustle: Sam Parr

**Idea 1. Sell the first ads yourself, then hire real salespeople.** Parr's first ad was $4,600 in June 2016, from a cold email to Wealthfront. In 2016, $100k of $400k revenue came from ads, at about 100k subscribers. MFM-2025 [14:16 to 15:31]. In 2017 the business had 250k subscribers and $2.2M revenue ($1.8M ads, $400k events), and "my big learning that year was hiring a sales team". MFM-2025 [18:16 to 18:33]. He sold "the first maybe $100,000 worth of ads" himself, then scaled with a sales team whose buttoned-up style he disliked but which "worked perfectly". MFM-2022 [17:45 to 17:57].

**Idea 2. Grow with content people share; buy ads only after you know retention.** The Hustle grew on "crazy blog posts", with 500k to 1M site visitors a month. MFM-2025 [14:16]. Parr spent about $1M on ads in 2018 (500k subscribers, $5.1M revenue, about $1M of it from events). He later regretted spending far less than Morning Brew. He also warns: people now "buy ads way earlier for their products… a huge mistake". MFM-2025 [26:26 to 30:01]. Lieberman envied The Hustle's welcome email. MFM-2025 [01:15].

**Idea 3. A paid product inside your content competence (Trends).** Trends was "basically a $300 a year subscription" (a weekly email plus a Facebook group), launched in 2019. Parr learned "the importance of building $300 up front versus monthly". MFM-2025 [37:20]. His rule for products beyond ads: they must sit within the core competency of content, or "it will almost always fail". He still says "make money via advertising, which is the right thing to do". MFM-2025 [57:58 to 58:21].

**Idea 4. Sell to a strategic buyer who wants your audience.** On the HubSpot deal, Parr tweeted "Most ad 1st media companies are dying." He "always thought a b2b SAAS biz should buy us". TechCrunch, 4 Feb 2021 (quotes Parr's tweets): https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/04/hubspot-acquires-media-startup-the-hustle/ . Rief's read on HubSpot: "they cared about our users", not an extra $10M of profit. MFM-2025 [50:12 to 50:37]. Parr did not use a banker for HubSpot. An earlier banker-led process had produced an all-stock offer from Vice, which he declined. MFM-2025 [49:03 to 49:39].

**The sale.** HubSpot announced the acquisition on 3 to 4 Feb 2021 and did not disclose the price. Axios (scoop, 3 Feb 2021) reported it at about $27M, citing a source; TechCrunch repeated this. HubSpot said the flagship newsletter had 1.5M subscribers. Parr: "I'm taking it to the grave!" (TechCrunch, above). Revenue at sale, from Parr's own year-by-year account: 2019 about $8M revenue ($640k events), $200k profit, $1.6M cash flow, 1.2M subscribers. 2020: $12M revenue, about $3M cash, 1.5M subscribers. MFM-2025 [30:43], [42:19 to 42:45]. On those numbers, the reported $27M is about 2.3x 2020 revenue. That multiple is my arithmetic on a secondary price, not a reported figure. LOI to close took 90 days. MFM-2025 [46:04]. The negotiation lesson ("price is primary and fit is the deal breaker") and the "deal doula" advice come from MFM #153, 12 Feb 2021 [20:41 to 24:24]: https://podscripts.co/podcasts/my-first-million/153-selling-the-hustle-for-millions-how-the-hustle-hubspot-deal-happened

## 4. beehiiv: Tyler Denk (ex-Morning Brew growth engineer)

**Idea 1. Paid recommendations (Boosts) are cost-per-subscriber acquisition, and a revenue line for the recommender.** Boosts launched on 27 Apr 2023: "You choose the price you're willing to pay for a new subscriber". You pay only for "real, active, and engaged subscribers", and an example offer is "$2.50 CPA". Recommenders earn by showing paid recommendations to their own new sign-ups. Denk, primary, beehiiv product post: https://product.beehiiv.com/p/introducing-boosts . Free recommendations were later revamped "so newsletters can recommend other newsletters". The Current, 11 Aug 2026: https://www.thecurrent.com/opinion/why-beehiiv-is-launching-programmatic-ads-ceo-tyler-denk

**Idea 2. A network gives small lists access to advertisers, at network rates and formats.** The ad network formally launched in September 2023. Within months, "over a thousand newsletters" had earned "over a million dollars in payouts". In February 2024, beehiiv guaranteed at least one ad opportunity a month to Scale-plan users. Denk, 5 Feb 2024, primary: https://product.beehiiv.com/p/ad-network-monetization-guarantee . beehiiv also bought the Swapstack ad marketplace in 2023. By January 2026 the network paid out "more than $1 million monthly", across CPC and CPM campaigns, and beehiiv was doubling its ad-sales team. PPC Land, 8 Jan 2026 (secondary, company announcement): https://ppc.land/beehiiv-doubles-ad-sales-team-as-newsletter-monetization-heats-up/ . PPC Land also cites network CPCs of "$1-$5 per click", averaging $2.50 (secondary, same URL).

**Idea 3. Programmatic is for scale publishers; independents do sales and writing themselves.** Denk: enterprise publishers have "a separation of church and state" between editorial and monetisation, while independents do both. Programmatic "very publisher friendly" for the former. He also said "The ad network is going to be the story over the next two years." The Current, 11 Aug 2026 (primary Q&A, URL above).

**Idea 4. The metric that matters: do publishers earn more than they pay?** In June 2024: 20,000 active publishers, of whom 20 to 25% monetised in the prior 30 days. ARR was $10.2M. Denk's north star is "how many of our users are earning more revenue than they are paying us". Latka interview write-up, 24 Jun 2024 (Denk's figures in interview): https://getlatka.com/interviews/beehiiv-tyler-denk-2024 . Reuters reported (via a secondary summary) that beehiiv expected about $50M revenue in 2026, after a $34M run-rate at the end of 2025, with "over $45 million earned by creators". Media Confidential, 21 Jan 2026 (secondary summary of Reuters): https://mediaconfidential.blogspot.com/2026/01/substack-challenger-beehiiv-expects.html . PPC Land, 8 Jan 2026, gives "more than $35 million", so the cumulative figure is inconsistent between sources.

**Idea 5. Referral rewards must be cheap to fulfil and worth one share.** On Milk Road's referral program, Denk notes that one referral unlocked a PDF report. Getting it was "a lot less time and effort" than asking around for a copy. Quoted in TheyGotAcquired, updated 9 Jan 2023 (secondary quoting Denk's blog): https://theygotacquired.com/content/milk-road-acquired-by-bitfo/ . Denk was Morning Brew's first "growth engineer" in 2017. MFM-2025 [18:49 to 19:24].

## 5. Lenny's Newsletter: Lenny Rachitsky

**Idea 1. He said no to ads in the newsletter and sent the demand to the podcast.** "I've always had a lot of demand to run ads in the newsletter," he said, but he did not want to. When he launched the podcast (first episode June 2022), he contacted those companies. In January 2024 the newsletter (574,000 subscribers, many paid) and the podcast each brought in more than $500,000 a year. CNBC by Gili Malinsky, 11 Jan 2024, read via NBC Los Angeles syndication (cnbc.com was not reachable): https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/business/money-report/42-year-old-brings-in-an-average-of-41000-a-month-from-his-podcast-and-works-on-it-just-5-hours-a-week/3309318/ . Companion piece, 15 Jan 2024: https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/business/money-report/42-year-old-brings-in-more-than-1-million-a-year-from-newsletter-podcast-my-advice-is-just-stop-tweeting-and-do-the-work/3312357/

**Idea 2. A premium price, justified by extra value.** About 10 months after starting the free letter, he set the paid tier at $15 a month ("more than Netflix") and added a hand-picked private Slack, starting with 30 members. After 10 months free and six weeks paid he had about 13K free subscribers, about 450 paid, and about $56K ARR. First Round Review, 15 Apr 2026 (profile with direct quotes): https://review.firstround.com/reluctantly-influential-inside-lenny-rachitskys-demandingly-chill-life/

**Idea 3. Partners pay in kind to paid readers (Product Pass), not by buying ad slots.** From February 2025, annual subscribers got a free year of partner products (Granola, Notion, Superhuman, Linear, Perplexity): https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/announcing-the-greatest-product-bundle (4 Feb 2025). By July 2025 the bundle was "worth over $25,000, for just $200-400/year", with a new $400 Insider tier for priority access: https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/productpass (29 Jul 2025, primary). When Perplexity's offer launched, it was "the biggest growth day in the history of his newsletter" (First Round, 15 Apr 2026). For us this is a model to study, not to copy: any partner perk must stay a flat arrangement with no per-lead element (D6).

**Idea 4. Paid ads don't build a newsletter; word of mouth does.** "Running Facebook or X ads are worthless." Word-of-mouth growth "dwarfs ads", even when ads are ROI-positive. Guest posts on established blogs worked. First Round Review, 15 Apr 2026 (URL above).

**Idea 5. Saying no protects quality; he cut frequency without losing readers.** "So I try to stay close to that, which is a lot of saying no." He reduced from four written letters a month to two to four, and "his audience actually appreciates it". He goes over each letter about 50 times before publishing. First Round Review, 15 Apr 2026. Scale: 1M subscribers in March 2025 (The Rebooting, 6 Mar 2025: https://www.therebooting.com/p/the-rise-of-lenny ). In March 2026 he confirmed "1.2 million" on Lenny's Podcast, 12 Mar 2026 [00:22]: https://podscripts.co/podcasts/lennys-podcast-product-career-growth/how-i-built-a-1m-subscriber-newsletter-and-top-10-tech-podcast-lenny-rachitsky

**Not found:** I found no source for sold-out sponsorships, a sponsor waiting list or published price increases at Lenny's (see "Could not verify"). Podcast sponsorship is handled by enquiry email in the show notes.

## 6. Others, each with one distinct lesson

**Milk Road (Shaan Puri, Ben Levy): fast growth, one hungry seller, an early sale.** Puri: "we were fully sold out for months on end" through the crypto bear market, with one young salesperson. MFM-2022 [17:16]. Growth went from 0 to 250k subscribers in about 10 months. Facebook ads brought "more than 150,000 subscribers at a cost of $1.00-$1.30 per subscriber", per growth marketer Matt McGarry (secondary via TheyGotAcquired, 9 Jan 2023, URL above). The newsletter was sold to Bitfo in December 2022 for an undisclosed price that included equity (same source). The buyers valued its search demand and diversified (non-crypto) advertisers. MFM, "Breaking: Shaan sells the Milk Road", 16 Dec 2022 [15:21 to 15:42]: https://podscripts.co/podcasts/my-first-million/breaking-shaan-sells-the-milk-road-a-conversation-with-the-buyers . A third-party data newsletter reported a primary ad at $3,000 to $4,000 and a $30 to $45 CPM at 250k subscribers with a 40% open rate (good.beehiiv.com, 8 Jan 2024, secondary): https://good.beehiiv.com/p/milk-road-data

**TLDR (Dan Ni): insider writers, sponsored links, paid acquisition at a known cost.** The main letter has 1.6M subscribers, out of more than 7M across a dozen letters. "An ad can cost as much as $30,000 per day to run." Revenue topped eight figures "last year" (the year before the profile, so 2024) and the company is profitable. Growth is about 50/50 organic and paid, and acquiring a subscriber "costs between $5 and $10" via ads. His rule for writers: "Write for somebody that works in the industry." Inc. profile by Rob Verger, republished 15 Dec 2025 (secondary with Ni quotes): https://inc-1-smart-business-story.beehiiv.com/p/the-man-silicon-valley-reads-every-morning

**Van Trump Report (Kevin Van Trump): a small, rich niche, paid, plus a conference.** It has "30 to 40,000" paid subscribers at about $600 a year, and the founder says "we've never advertised one time". Its conference (FarmCon) grew out of the letter. MFM #603, 1 Jul 2024 [00:00 to 01:16], [07:04], [11:08] (figures are the founder's own, spoken): https://podscripts.co/podcasts/my-first-million/the-simple-newsletter-making-20m-a-year-with-1-employee

**Chartr: a strategic buyer in the reader's money category.** Robinhood bought Chartr for its Sherwood Media unit, with no terms disclosed. Robinhood newsroom, 21 Dec 2023 (primary): https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/sherwood-media-portfolio-grows-with-chartr-limited-acquisition . This fits the pattern of HubSpot buying The Hustle: buyers whose customers are the readers.

## Agreements and disagreements

**Where they agree**
- **Content quality sets the ad price.** Rief: weak content means $3,000 ads instead of $50,000. Lieberman: they printed the letter daily and marked it up by hand. VandeHei: most stories are too long or unnecessary. Lenny: he reads each letter about 50 times. All four tie revenue to editorial standards (MFM-2025 [21:25], [55:28]; Digiday 5 Apr 2017; First Round 15 Apr 2026).
- **Ads should read like the letter and be labelled.** Morning Brew uses in-house copywriters and "Sponsored By" labels. Axios uses one short native unit labelled "A message from". beehiiv's network inserts ads as blocks inside the editor. No one argues for banners in email. Axios explicitly rejects banners and programmatic.
- **Engaged, niche audiences earn higher CPMs.** Lieberman ("the more niche, the better"), Brew's B2B verticals, TLDR's industry insiders and the Van Trump Report's $600 farm and commodities letter all point the same way.
- **Referrals and cross-promotion first.** Brew's tiered referrals, Milk Road's single-PDF reward, beehiiv recommendations and Boosts, and Lenny's word of mouth all came before or instead of large paid budgets.
- **Ad revenue is cyclical.** Rief sold partly because "ads businesses are quite volatile". COVID took about 30% of Brew's booked revenue in weeks. Rief adds "every day with ads, it's another grind" (MFM-2022 [14:08], speaker inferred). This argues for annual and multi-month flat packages booked in advance.
- **Buyers pay for audience and trust, not for ad profit.** HubSpot bought The Hustle's users, Robinhood bought Chartr, and Bitfo bought Milk Road for its search demand. Media buyers (Axel Springer, Cox) paid about 4–5x revenue for profitable, growing brands.

**Where they disagree (and which side fits us)**
- **Paid acquisition.** Brew spent $6–7M a year. Parr now calls early ad-buying a mistake. Lenny calls FB/X ads "worthless". TLDR runs about 50% paid at $5–10 per subscriber, and Milk Road paid $1–1.30. *Fit for us:* we have no budget before revenue (hard rule 5), so referrals, recommendations and partner cross-promotion come first. Paid acquisition comes later, only with known retention.
- **Ads vs. reader revenue.** Brew and The Hustle were ad-first, and Parr still calls advertising "the right thing to do". Lenny refuses newsletter ads and earns from paid subscriptions, podcast ads and in-kind partner perks. Axios bet on Pro reaching 50% of revenue and got under 1% in year 1. *Fit for us:* flat sponsorship first (it is what we sell and what is exited). Paid guides and calls are a smaller second line. Partner perks are allowed only as flat deals (D6).
- **Pricing unit.** Brew prices per thousand opens. beehiiv's network uses CPM and CPC. Axios sells flat one-week exclusives with premium weeks. *Fit for us:* Axios-style flat time-boxed exclusivity matches D6 and a small, high-value list. CPM and CPC terms do not apply to us; we only report reach.
- **Ad load.** Morning Brew now runs about six paid elements in a daily issue. Axios in 2017 ran one short unit, sold as a weekly exclusive. Lenny runs none in the newsletter. *Fit for us:* a premium twice-weekly letter sits closer to Axios and Lenny, with very few sponsored elements per issue (the number is for the CEO to decide in the §3 plan).
- **Direct response vs. brand.** The Hustle used an efficient inside-sales team selling direct response. Brew went to New York agencies for brand budgets of up to $5M. Axios sold only to blue-chip brands. *Fit for us:* banks, golf resorts, wine and hotels buy brand and positioning, which suits flat packages and title sponsorship over click-based deals.
- **One letter or many.** Brew went horizontal into B2B verticals because distinct audiences are "easier to explain and sell" (PostUp's Sibson in Digiday, 7 Feb 2019). The Hustle stayed with one letter plus a paid product. *Fit for us:* one letter (D74) for now, with segments shown to sponsors by reader country.
- **Funding.** Parr would not raise at all. Rief says raise "as little as humanly possible". Axios raised $57m. *Fit for us:* no outside capital, which is already the plan.
- **Banker or not when selling.** Parr sold to HubSpot without one, after a bad banker-led process. Brew ran an 11-month process and regretted selling partly out of fear of losing the only offer. *Fit for us:* prepare a clean data room early so the sale does not depend on one offer.
