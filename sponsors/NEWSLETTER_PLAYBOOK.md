## 1. What they agree on, and where they disagree

**They agree on six things:**
1. **Content quality sets the ad price.** Rief: weak content means $3,000 ads instead of $50,000 (MFM 2025 [21:25]). Lenny reads each letter about 50 times.
2. **One native format, written in the letter's voice and clearly labelled.** No banners in email (Axios, Morning Brew, Oshinsky, beehiiv's block ads).
3. **Niche, engaged audiences earn the highest prices** (Lieberman, TLDR, Van Trump, Paved's ladder).
4. **Referrals and cross-promotion come before paid acquisition** (Morning Brew's tiers, Milk Road's single-PDF reward, beehiiv recommendations, Lenny's word of mouth).
5. **Ad revenue is cyclical.** COVID took about 30% of Morning Brew's booked revenue within weeks. So book in advance, in multi-month packages.
6. **Buyers pay for audience and trust.** HubSpot bought The Hustle, Robinhood bought Chartr, and Watches of Switzerland bought Hodinkee: each bought a defined audience.

**They disagree on six things; the side that fits us is in brackets:**
- **Price unit.** Per open (Morning Brew), CPC (beehiiv) or flat time-boxed exclusives (Axios). **(Axios: flat, by week or month, exclusive by category. It is D6 by design.)**
- **Ad load.** About six paid elements a day at Morning Brew (2 Nov 2026 issue), one unit at Axios in 2017, none in Lenny's letter. **(Close to Axios: one sponsor element per letter, two at most.)**
- **Short native ads versus long sponsored pieces.** Not Boring, Monocle and Mansion Global sell whole features at 4–7× a slot. **(Both: the block as the base, a rare sponsored deep dive at the top.)**
- **Scale versus scarcity.** Morning Brew bought reach. Puck, Monocle and the Wine Experience sell a small, rich room. **(Scarcity: we will never have Morning Brew's scale, and our buyers are banks, resorts and developers.)**
- **Paid acquisition.** Brew spent $6–7M a year; Parr regretted his early ad spend; Lenny calls ads "worthless". **(No paid acquisition before revenue, hard rule 5. Recommendations and partner cross-promotion first.)**
- **Print and physical things.** Monocle, Wine Spectator, TRD and Robb Report keep print as the luxury signal. **(Not now. One printed object a year, the Portugal 100, once it pays for itself.)**

## 2. Quality guardrails

- **How many ads.**
  - Axios: one native unit, sold weekly. Morning Brew early on: one primary ad plus "a secondary ad some days" of about 80 words (Rief, A Media Operator, S). Lenny: none in the letter.
  - HTSI caps branded content at one or two brands per category and four or five campaigns live.
  - Oshinsky: readers leave dedicated sponsor sends much faster than the normal letter.
- **The ratio.** No one publishes a fixed ad-to-content ratio. In practice the sponsor element is 100–175 words in a letter of 800–1,500 (Axios ad about 175 words, 2017; Brew native copy 100–150 words).
- **Native done right.**
  - The letter's own writers draft the ad and the sponsor approves it (Morning Brew).
  - It sits in the same type, with a label that cannot be missed: "A message from" (Axios), "Presented by" and "Sponsored by" (Morning Brew), "Sponsored content" with copy and logo (TRD).
  - Monocle's "X" label was criticised as too light (Press Gazette 2017). Our label stays explicit (hard rule 4; Código da Publicidade art. 8).
- **What they refused, and why.**
  - Shanken refused to be "a promotional arm" and kept a "not recommended" band.
  - TRD keeps stories advertisers want pulled, and runs events without pay-to-play panels.
  - Packy accepts under a third of deep-dive requests, using a written test.
  - Lenny said no to newsletter ads altogether.
  - Axios refused banners and programmatic.
  - Kelly: Puck is as selective about advertisers as about stories.
- **The warning.** Pay-to-enter awards invite the 2008 Wine Spectator hoax. Any paid "badge" needs a check of the place or its documents, and published refusals.

| **5,000–20,000** | Median main slot $300 for 5k–20k lists (Book a Sponsor, Oct 2026, S). Not Boring at about 10–40k: $3–5k per slot, $20k per deep dive (2021, S). Oshinsky floor "at least $500" at 10k (unverified). | $15–50 per 1,000 opens (Oshinsky); niche $30–75, premium/executive $75+ (Paved). |
| **About 100,000 and more** | Morning Brew at about 1M: $60–70 per 1,000 opens (2019). Milk Road at 250k: $3,000–4,000 per primary ad, $30–45 CPM (S). Mansion Global: $3,680 for a week of 100% share of voice in the daily (2025). **When to raise prices.**
- Packy raised "in line with audience size" and charged more for Mondays.
- Axios charged more in the weeks that mattered.
- Morning Brew sold out the first half of the year by February, then moved up from direct-response to brand budgets.
- Milk Road was "fully sold out for months on end", which is the moment to raise.
- Our rule: raise every quarter in line with subscribers, and immediately when the next quarter is 80% booked. Hold a waiting list rather than add a second element.

**Flat or CPM.** Every premium title in group 2 sells flat units: weeks, takeovers, features, events. CPM is how the open market compares prices, so we publish our price per 1,000 subscribers once we pass 5,000. We never sell per click or per lead (D6).

## 4. Products beyond the ad slot

- **Title sponsorship.** Not Boring's "Presenting Sponsor" for a year (Ramp, 2025); Axios's weekly exclusive; Mansion Global's 100% share of voice.
- **Sponsored deep dive or custom feature.** Not Boring $20k vs $3–5k (S); Mansion Global $25,000 vs $3,680 a week. Our version is "A história, apresentada por", under Packy's rules.
- **Series.** A theme over several letters (golf weeks, Douro harvest) bought as a calendar, not as slots.
- **Events, small and editor-hosted.**
  - Monocle: about 200 seats at $2,600 (Lisbon 2026); events +87% in 2025.
  - Puck: dinners for its $250 tier.
  - TRD: 3,000+ at its forum, editors moderating.
  - Wine Experience: about $420 for one night, about $2,695 for the weekend (S).
- **Awards and rankings.** TRD's Data Book with 80+ rankings; Robb Report's "Best of the Best"; Wine Spectator's ratings and awards. Ours is the Portugal 100 (D69): one yearly reveal with one presenting sponsor, never paid entry.
- **Licensing a result.** Shanken charges $1,000/yr per brand to use a rating in its marketing. A later line for us, once the CEO and a lawyer agree; never paid entry into the ranking.
- **Commerce.** Hodinkee's limited editions worked; owning stock did not. Monocle's shops and café grew 22%, from "brand-in-residence" sponsorship. For us: products made with partners (a wine, a guide), never inventory.
- **Paid tiers.** Puck ($100/$250), Axios Pro ($599; about 2% of revenue in year one), The Hustle Trends ($300), Lenny ($200–400 with the Product Pass). For us: paid guides and calls (CLAUDE.md), and a paid tier only beyond 20,000 subscribers.
- **Jobs boards and classifieds.** About ¼ of a native ad (Oshinsky). Not for us: our classifieds would be property listings, which D9 and D14 exclude.
- **Paid recommendations.** beehiiv Boosts about $1.63–2.37 per subscriber; SparkLoop $2–7, with 20% + about 3.5% to the platform. Earning money by recommending other letters is a per-subscriber fee: a **CEO decision** under D6.
- **Data products.** The yearly Data Book (TRD), the Shanken Ratings Network database (425,000+ ratings). For us: the index and Portugal 100 as the asset, and the monthly sponsor report as the renewal tool (prompt 51).

## 5. Growth: what worked first

1. **Referrals with a reward that is cheap to fulfil.**
   - Morning Brew's tiers: 3 referrals unlocked a Sunday edition that could not be bought. More than 50,000 readers referred at least three.
   - Milk Road gave one PDF report for one referral.
2. **Cross-promotion and recommendations:** beehiiv Recommendations; swaps with similar letters; guest posts on established blogs (Lenny).
3. **Content people share.** The Hustle's blog brought 0.5–1M visitors a month.
4. **Paid acquisition, once retention is known.**
   - Reported costs per subscriber: McGarry rates $2 on Meta as world class and his own campaign cost $3.86; Milk Road paid $1.00–1.30 (S); TLDR pays $5–10 (S); beehiiv Boosts cost $1.63–2.37.
   - Payback works only if revenue per subscriber is high. Ours is high per head, but the list is small.
5. **For us:**
   - partner cross-promotion: the agency's mailing, the developers' lists, the diaspora chambers (prompt 48);
   - the Portugal 100 and the index as press hooks;
   - a referral reward: the Portugal 100 PDF before publication.

   Paid acquisition starts only after the first sponsor has paid (hard rule 5). Even then, under $3 a subscriber, with a three-month payback.

What raised the multiple:
- documented sell-through and renewals (Morning Brew above 70%);
- a defined, rich audience;
- independence rules written down from day one (Wine Spectator, TRD);
- a strategic buyer in the field (HubSpot, Watches of Switzerland, Robinhood);
- a clean data room, so the sale does not hang on one offer (Rief's 11-month process).

**For us:** the buyer is a portal, an agency network or a media group (CLAUDE.md). Give them:
- 12 months of the monthly sponsor reports;
- renewal rates;
- subscribers by country and wealth signals;
- the index and its method;
- the editorial rules.
