## Common to all editions

**1. Can I buy property in Portugal if I don't live there?**
Yes. Portugal does not restrict property purchases by foreigners or non-residents. You need a Portuguese tax number (NIF) before signing; depending on where you live, you may also need a tax representative **[check: rules for EU vs non-EU residents]**. A partner lawyer can get the NIF and run the purchase for you. **[talk-lawyer]**

**2. What does it cost besides the price?**
Expect property transfer tax (IMT, which depends on price and use), stamp duty of 0.8% of the price **[check]**, notary and land registry fees, and your lawyer. With a mortgage, add stamp duty on the loan **[check: 0.6%]** and bank fees. Our free guide works it out on real examples. **[guide]**

**3. Can I get a mortgage as a non-resident?**
Yes. Portuguese banks lend to non-residents, usually for a lower share of the price than for residents **[check: Banco de Portugal LTV limits and usual practice]**, and they assess your income where you live. Ask a partner bank what you could borrow. **[talk-bank]**

**4. Do I have to be in Portugal to sign?**
No. You can buy through a power of attorney, usually given to a lawyer, signed abroad with the formalities Portugal requires **[check: apostille or consulate certification]**. Many buyers only come for the keys. **[talk-lawyer]**

**5. What is a CPCV?**
The promissory purchase contract signed before the deed. It fixes the price, the date and the deposit **[check: usual deposit range]**. If the buyer walks away, they usually lose the deposit; if the seller does, they usually pay it back doubled **[check: Código Civil, art. 442]**. Have it reviewed before signing. **[talk-lawyer]**

**6. What taxes do I pay every year?**
IMI, a municipal property tax between 0.3% and 0.45% of the property's tax value for urban property, set by each municipality **[check: CIMI, art. 112]**. Above a high total tax value, AIMI also applies **[check: threshold]**. A partner tax adviser can calculate yours. **[talk-tax]**

**7. Can I rent it out?**
Yes, long-term or as short-term local accommodation (alojamento local). Short-term rental rules changed in 2023 and 2024 and vary by municipality **[check: current regime]**. Rental income is taxed in Portugal and may have to be declared where you live. **[talk-tax]**

**8. How do I send the money?**
Through your bank or a specialised transfer provider; the difference in exchange rate on a large amount can be thousands of euros. Keep proof of where the funds come from: the bank and the notary will ask for it. Compare with a partner transfer provider. **[talk-fx]**

**9. I inherited a house in Portugal. What now?**
The heirs register the inheritance and decide together whether to keep, sell or rent. Spouses, children and parents are exempt from inheritance stamp duty on the transfer **[check: Código do Imposto do Selo, art. 6]**, but registration and other formalities remain. A partner lawyer can handle it from abroad. **[talk-lawyer]**

**10. Is now a good time to buy?**
We don't give investment advice. The facts: house prices in Portugal rose 17.6% in 2025, the highest increase in the INE series. What matters is your budget, region and plans. Tell us what you want and our partner agency will show you what exists. **[agency]**

**11. Can you find me a house?**
Yes. Tell us the region, budget and timing. Our licensed partner agency contacts you, free of charge, and can organise viewings for when you are in Portugal. **[agency]**

**12. Is this financial, legal or tax advice?**
No. We compile information from official sources so you understand the rules. For your own case, talk to a professional: we can put you in touch with our partners. **[talk-lawyer]**
