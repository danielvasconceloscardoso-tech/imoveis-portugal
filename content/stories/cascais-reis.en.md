In Cascais there is a well with a float in it. The float is connected to a high-precision clock and to a cylinder that records, as a graph, every rise and fall of the sea. This is the [Cascais tide gauge](fact:cascais-royal-1870), installed in 1882 and moved about 30 meters in 1900. It was linked to King Carlos's oceanography laboratory, it still works, and it can be visited by appointment.

The king's love of the sea came from his father. In 1870, King Luís and Queen Maria Pia set up the royal summer residence in the governor's houses of the Citadel of [Cascais](place:cascais), adapted by Joaquim Possidónio Narciso da Silva. In 1878, for his son's 15th birthday, King Luís gave him the schooner Nautilus, and on the same day Cascais saw the first trial of electric lighting in Portugal.[[cascais-royal-1870]]

The court brought the fashion for sea bathing, and the small fishing settlement became the [first beach of the kingdom](fact:cascais-veraneio). The fishermen had to give up part of Praia da Ribeira to the bathers; it is now called Praia dos Pescadores. The cove next to it, then known as Boca do Asno, was Queen Maria Pia's choice for the princes' sea baths, and it was there, in 1900, that Queen Amélia saved a fisherman from drowning. Today it is called Praia da Rainha.[[cascais-royal-1870]]

As king, Carlos promoted sailing, rowing and swimming from Praia dos Pescadores, and Cascais became the most prestigious regatta ground in the country. In 1896 he set up Portugal's first marine biology laboratory in the Citadel. There is a photograph of him from 1900 rowing, and smoking, in front of the Casino da Praia.[[cascais-royal-1870]]

With the Republic, the palace began to host the presidents, and Óscar Carmona lived there from 1928 to 1945. Restored in 2007 and 2008, it is today an official residence of the President, with guided tours. The statue of King Carlos by Luís Valadares, from 2008, puts him aboard the yacht Amélia, binoculars in hand, facing the bay.[[cascais-royal-1870]]

The same year, the Casa das Histórias Paula Rego opened, designed by Eduardo Souto de Moura. The painter chose the architect and he chose the site, a fenced-off forest with a clearing in the middle, and made the concrete red to stand against the green of the trees.[[pritzker-souto-2011]]
