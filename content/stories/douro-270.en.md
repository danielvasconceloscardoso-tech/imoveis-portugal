In a Christie's auction catalog from 1768, a Vintage Port appears for the first time. The vintage was 1765.[[ivdp-milestones]] A few years earlier, the wine of the Douro had been in crisis. All through that century, English, Dutch and German merchants based in Porto bought it and shipped it out through the city's harbor.[[ivdp-milestones]]

Success in export markets had brought imitations. Wines from all over were [doctored to pass as Port](fact:ivdp-1756): elderberries for color, darker Spanish wine, sugar to hide poor brandy. By around 1750 the quality was in question, and in 1754 the English buyers did not buy wine from the growers.

The answer came on September 10, 1756, in a charter from King José I that created the Companhia Geral da Agricultura das Vinhas do Alto Douro and, with it, the Demarcated Region. From then on there was a register of vineyard plots, maximum quantities for sale, prices set by the quality the Company's officials verified, and a ban on adding anything that would change the wine's profile. The Marquis of Pombal drew the model from his years as ambassador in London and Vienna.[[ivdp-1756]]

It was the world's first appellation of origin. France only defined the appellation d'origine in the first quarter of the 20th century.[[ivdp-1756]]

On the ground, the Pombaline demarcations were carried out between 1757 and 1761, and Queen Maria I enlarged the area in 1788. In 1792, work was completed to destroy the Cachão da Valeira, the rock that closed the river near [São João da Pesqueira](place:sao-joao-da-pesqueira), and the Douro became navigable all the way to Spain.[[ivdp-milestones]]

Today the [Demarcated Region](fact:ivdp-region) covers 247,420 hectares, from the Baixo Corgo, where vines cover about 29% of the land, to the Cima Corgo and the Douro Superior, which runs to the border. UNESCO listed the Alto Douro Wine Region as a World Heritage Site in 2001.[[ivdp-milestones]] In [Peso da Régua](place:peso-da-regua), the old Company building has been the seat of the Douro Museum since December 20, 2008.[[ivdp-milestones]] For centuries, the casks came down the river to [Vila Nova de Gaia](place:vila-nova-de-gaia) on rabelo boats. They left the scene with the Carrapatelo dam, built between 1964 and 1971, the first of five on the Portuguese Douro; the train, and then tanker trucks, took over.[[ivdp-milestones]] The merchants whose accounts the charter also set straight were based in Gaia and in [Porto](place:porto).[[ivdp-1756]]
