Eduardo Souto de Moura enrolled at the School of Fine Arts in [Porto](place:porto) to study sculpture. He credits a [meeting with Donald Judd, in Zurich](fact:pritzker-souto-2011), for his switch to architecture. Born in Porto in 1952, he worked for Álvaro Siza for five years while still a student, opened his own office in 1980 and received the Pritzker in 2011, the second for a Portuguese architect.

The first had gone to Siza, in 1992. He was born in [Matosinhos](place:matosinhos) on June 25, 1933, and built his first works before he graduated: four houses in his hometown. He opened his office in Porto in 1954 and began teaching at the university in 1966.[[pritzker-siza-1992]] At Leça da Palmeira, on the edge of the Atlantic, two of his works are now more than sixty years old: the [Boa Nova restaurant, from 1963, and the swimming pool, from 1966](fact:pritzker-siza-1992).

In [Évora](place:evora), in 1977, right after the revolution, the city government commissioned the Malagueira quarter from him: 1,200 low-cost houses, one or two stories high, every one with a courtyard. In July 1986, Casabella magazine saw in his work a return of the heroic spirit of modern architecture.[[pritzker-siza-1992]]

For the municipal stadium in [Braga](place:braga), finished for Euro 2004, Souto de Moura had a mountainside blasted. Nearly a million and a half cubic yards of granite were broken up and crushed to make the concrete, and one end of the stadium stands against a hundred-foot rock face. He told the Pritzker jury that breaking down the mountain and making concrete from the stone had been a drama. The jury wrote that his work is of our time but also carries echoes of architectural traditions.[[pritzker-souto-2011]]

In [Amares](place:amares), between 1989 and 1997, he turned the 12th-century monastery of Santa Maria do Bouro into a state inn, and he remembers walls more than four feet thick. In Porto, he designed the Burgo Tower, completed in 2007: two buildings side by side, one vertical and one horizontal.[[pritzker-souto-2011]]

The two names cross in Matosinhos, at the roundabout by the sea, where Janet Echelman's net, from 2005, had Souto de Moura as the architect of the ensemble.[[echelman-she-changes]] Siza's pool lies on the other side of the port of Leixões.
