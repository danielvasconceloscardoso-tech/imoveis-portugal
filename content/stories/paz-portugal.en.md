In the table of the [2026 Global Peace Index](fact:gpi-2026-portugal), Portugal's row reads 7, Portugal, 1.427. Austria, on the row above, has 1.421. Singapore, on the row below, has 1.435. That is six thousandths one way and eight the other, and the scoring works the way golf does: the lowest number wins.

At the top is Iceland, at 1.161, first for the 19th year running. Between Iceland and Portugal sit New Zealand, Switzerland, Slovenia, Ireland and Austria; after Singapore come Finland and Japan, which closes out the top ten. Next to each name, an arrow tells the year: Portugal's climbs one place, with the score improving by 0.020; Austria and Singapore each slip one place; Japan climbs three. Denmark is just outside, 11th at 1.504.[[gpi-2026-portugal]] Readers of the Swiss edition will find Switzerland in 3rd, at 1.363. Further down the same table, Spain, Portugal's only land neighbor, is 27th at 1.654; Germany is 28th at 1.657; Italy 35th at 1.712; and the United Kingdom 39th at 1.73.[[gpi-2026-portugal]]

The index is made in Sydney by the [Institute for Economics & Peace](fact:gpi-2026-method) and reached its 20th edition in June 2026. It covers 163 countries and territories, home to 99.7% of the world's population, and measures each on 23 indicators, some qualitative and some quantitative, grouped under safety and security within society, ongoing conflict, and militarization.[[gpi-2026-method]]

The year around that ranking was not a good one for the rest of the world. Average peacefulness worldwide fell by 0.7%, for the 12th year in a row, with 99 countries getting worse and 62 improving.[[gpi-2026-method]] Portugal is in the second group. In Western and Central Europe, the region that holds seven of the world's ten most peaceful countries, it ranks 6th.[[gpi-2026-method]]

The score belongs to the whole country, with no cities or neighborhoods. It reads the same in [Bragança](place:braganca) and in [Faro](place:faro), in [Funchal](place:funchal) and in [Ponta Delgada](place:ponta-delgada), and says nothing about any particular street. The tables for all 163 countries, indicator by indicator and with each one's arrow, are in the full report, which is free to read and linked in the sources at the foot of the page.
