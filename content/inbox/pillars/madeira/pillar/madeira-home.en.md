Over the 12 months to the second quarter of 2026, the median home sold in Funchal, Madeira's capital, cost €3,538 per square meter, 188% more than in the first quarter of 2016. Across the Madeira region, the median was €2,690.[[ine-m2-2026q2]][[ine-m2-history]] The figures come from INE, Portugal's national statistics office, and Imóveis Portugal last checked them on October 25, 2026. Madeira is part of Portugal, so a purchase there follows the country's tax codes, and you can buy as a non-resident.[[pt-nonresidents-buy]]

## Every municipality on Madeira has its own INE price

The Autonomous Region of Madeira has 11 municipalities: ten on Madeira itself and one, Porto Santo, on the neighboring island.[[caop-2025]] For each one, INE publishes the median value per square meter of family homes sold in the last 12 months, new and existing. A median is the middle sale, not a listing price and not a home: it tells you where your budget buys more space. The [house prices](/en/us/house-prices/) page draws each municipality's line since 2016.

For Ponta do Sol, INE's rent series has a figure only for 2021, so the page shows no rent or yield.[[ine-rent-history]] Gross yield is a year of median rent divided by the median price, before costs and tax. Past prices say nothing about the next ten years.

At the median, €400,000 is about 113 m² in Funchal, 140 m² in Santa Cruz, 193 m² in Machico, 214 m² in Calheta and 226 m² in Ponta do Sol.[[ine-m2-2026q2]] Type your own budget into [the map](/en/us/map/?budget=400000) to see every municipality on the island.

## Lisbon and Porto are Madeira's busiest routes

"How often would I actually get there?" Madeira airport handled 5,390,975 passengers in 2025, 2,257,216 of them on flights within Portugal.[[eurostat-lpma-2025]] In 2024, the latest year Eurostat publishes by route, 1,393,914 passengers flew between Madeira and Lisbon, and 606,548 between Madeira and Porto.[[eurostat-lpma-routes-2024]] The edition sections below give the routes from Luxembourg, France and Switzerland.

## Buying in Madeira, step by step

"I can't move to Funchal for three months to buy a flat." You don't need to.

1. **A tax number.** A legal representative can request your NIF online; no tax representative is needed to get it.[[pt-nif-nonresident]]
2. **Where to look.** The map narrows the municipality; first visits can be on video.
3. **Someone to sign for you.** A power of attorney signed at a Portuguese consulate, or before a notary where you live with the Hague Apostille, lets someone sign in your name.[[pt-poa-apostille]]
4. **Finance.** Banco de Portugal caps new mortgages at 90% of the value for your own permanent home and 80% for other homes, such as a vacation home.[[pt-ltv-limits]]
5. **The promissory contract (CPCV).** It usually comes with a deposit. If you default, the seller keeps it; if the seller defaults, you can claim double.[[pt-sinal-442]]
6. **The deed and the taxes.** IMT and stamp duty are the main purchase taxes; the next section runs the numbers.
7. **After the purchase.** If you live outside the EU and the EEA, you have 15 days to appoint a tax representative or join the tax authority's electronic notices.[[pt-tax-rep]][[pt-nif-nonresident]] Each year, IMI is 0.3% to 0.45% of the home's tax value, at the rate the municipality sets.[[pt-imi-rates]]

Each station has its page on [Step by step](/en/us/step-by-step/?door=buy&step=1).

## The cost of buying a €400,000 home in Madeira

If you aren't a Portuguese tax resident, the transfer tax (IMT) on a home is a flat 7.5%, with no exemption or reduction, unless an exception applies.[[pt-imt-nonresident-flat]] Stamp duty is 0.8%.[[pt-is-purchase]] On €400,000, that's €30,000 of IMT and €3,200 of stamp duty: €433,200 before the deed and registration. This page doesn't quote a deed cost for Madeira, because we haven't checked whether the national Casa Pronta price list applies on the island; a partner lawyer gives you the figure. The [total-cost calculator](/en/us/calculators/total-cost/?price=400000&resident=0) runs your own price.

The law has three exceptions to the flat rate.[[pt-imt-nonresident-exceptions]] One covers anyone who "has been considered a tax resident" in Portugal; another, anyone who becomes one within two years of the purchase. If you once lived in Portugal, how the first exception applies to you is a question for a partner lawyer or tax adviser, before you sign the promissory contract.

## Renting the home out

If you're not a Portuguese tax resident, rent from a home in Portugal is taxed in Portugal at 25%, with reductions for leases of five years or more.[[pt-rent-tax-nonresident]] Short-term rental has its own registration rules; we haven't checked how they apply in the autonomous region, so a partner lawyer confirms them for your address.

## Where you live changes the route and the paperwork

### Luxembourg

The Madeira–Luxembourg route carried 27,909 passengers in 2024.[[eurostat-lpma-routes-2024]] If you rent the home out, the Portugal–Luxembourg treaty lets Portugal tax the rent; Luxembourg exempts it but may count it when setting your rate.[[lu-pt-treaty-property]]

### France

In 2024, 79,943 passengers flew between Madeira and Paris-Orly, 35,812 to Beauvais, 28,811 to Charles de Gaulle and 37,043 to Lyon.[[eurostat-lpma-routes-2024]] A bank account opened in Portugal goes on form 3916 every year.[[fr-3916]]

### Switzerland

Zurich carried 54,800 passengers to and from Madeira in 2024, Basel-Mulhouse 31,967 and Geneva 26,853.[[eurostat-lpma-routes-2024]] €400,000 is about CHF 369,080 at the ECB reference rate of October 23, 2026.[[ecb-eur-chf]] 2nd-pillar money can go to your main residence abroad, not to a vacation home.[[ch-2nd-pillar-abroad]] Rent and gains may be taxed in Portugal; Switzerland exempts them but may apply the rate it would have used.[[ch-pt-treaty-property]]

### Brazil

€400,000 is about R$2.26 million at the ECB rate.[[ecb-eur-brl]] The home goes into the IRPF under Bens e Direitos, converted to dollars and then to reais at the Banco Central selling rate on the purchase date.[[br-bens-exterior]] Eurostat's 2024 route table has no Madeira–Brazil route.[[eurostat-lpma-routes-2024]]

### United States

€400,000 is about $447,720 at the ECB rate.[[ecb-eur-usd]] A Portuguese bank account counts toward the $10,000 FBAR threshold.[[us-fbar]] Eurostat's 2024 route table has no Madeira–US route; routes change by season, and airlines publish their own schedules.[[eurostat-lpma-routes-2024]]

### Israel

€400,000 is about ₪1.40 million at the ECB rate.[[ecb-eur-ils]] If you rent the home out, Portugal may tax the rent and Israel gives a credit for the Portuguese tax.[[il-pt-treaty-property]]

## Questions

**How much does a home in Madeira cost?**

INE's median for homes sold in the 12 months to mid-2026 is €3,538 per square meter in Funchal and €2,690 across the region. In Santa Cruz it's €2,853, in Machico €2,070, in Calheta €1,865 and in Ponta do Sol €1,767. These are medians of real sales, new and existing, not listing prices. The map shows every municipality.

**Can a foreigner buy property in Madeira?**

Yes. Madeira is part of Portugal, and non-residents buy under the same national tax codes, with a NIF and, if you can't be there, a power of attorney. If you aren't a Portuguese tax resident, IMT on a home is a flat 7.5%, plus 0.8% stamp duty, unless an exception applies. A partner lawyer can handle the steps from abroad.

**Have Madeira house prices gone up?**

Yes, in all five municipalities on this page. Between the first quarter of 2016 and the second quarter of 2026, the INE median per square meter rose 188% in Funchal, 221% in Santa Cruz, 172% in Machico, 136% in Calheta and 147% in Ponta do Sol. Past prices say nothing about the next ten years.

**Are there direct flights to Madeira from Paris or Geneva?**

There were in 2024: Eurostat counts 79,943 passengers on the Madeira–Paris-Orly route and 26,853 on Madeira–Geneva, plus Zurich, Basel-Mulhouse, Lyon and Luxembourg. Lisbon is the largest route, with 1.39 million passengers. Routes change by season, so the airlines' own schedules give the current flights.
