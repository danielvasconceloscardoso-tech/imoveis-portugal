## You can sign without flying in

"I can't fly to Portugal just to sign." Portuguese law doesn't ask you to. A power of attorney signed at a Portuguese consulate, or before a notary where you live with the Hague Apostille, lets the person you choose, often a lawyer, sign the promissory contract and the deed in your name (source: Portugal's consular services, checked October 24, 2026). You can still come over for the visit, the keys or both.

On the day, a Casa Pronta office does the deed and the land registration in one sitting, for €375, about $420 at the ECB reference rate of October 23 (Justiça.gov.pt, checked October 25, 2026). Casa Pronta asks for the usage license, or its waiver, and the energy certificate. Taxes and the lawyer's fees are separate.

## What's different from the US

A power of attorney from a US notary needs the apostille; the Consulate General of Portugal in Newark signs them too, with no apostille. The US is outside the EU, so after the deed you appoint a tax representative in Portugal or sign up for the tax office's electronic notifications. And if you open a Portuguese bank account for the deposit, it counts toward the FBAR threshold along with your other foreign accounts.
