You can sign without flying in

"I can't fly to Portugal just to sign." Portuguese law doesn't ask you to. A power of attorney signed at a Portuguese consulate, or before a notary where you live with the Hague Apostille, lets the person you choose, often a lawyer, sign the promissory contract and the deed in your name (source: Portugal's consular services, checked October 24, 2026). You can still come over for the visit, the keys or both.

On the day, a Casa Pronta office does the deed and the land registration in one sitting, for €375 (Justiça.gov.pt, checked October 25, 2026). Casa Pronta asks for the usage license, or its waiver, and the energy certificate. Taxes and the lawyer's fees are separate.

What's different from France

The Consulate General of Portugal in Paris handles notary acts, powers of attorney included, and no apostille is needed there. Before a French notary, the power of attorney carries the Hague Apostille. Because you live in the EU, a tax representative is optional after the deed. If you open a Portuguese bank account to pay the deposit, as a French tax resident you declare it every year on form 3916, even if it sits unused.
