## This week's home

Our partner agency's first home goes here once we have its written permission. Until then, here's the bill for a €300,000 home if you aren't a Portuguese tax resident and you pay cash:

- Price: €300,000
- Transfer tax (IMT), 7.5% flat for non-residents: €22,500
- Stamp duty, 0.8%: €2,400
- Deed and registration at Casa Pronta: €375
- Total cost with taxes, deed and registration: €325,275

The law has exceptions to the 7.5%. If you become a Portuguese tax resident within two years of the purchase, you ask for the difference back. Another exception covers anyone who "has been considered a tax resident" in Portugal. How that applies if you once lived in Portugal and then moved abroad depends on your case, so it's a question for a partner lawyer before you sign the promissory contract.

Run it with your own price: https://imoveisportugal.lu/en/lu/calculators/total-cost/?price=300000&resident=0&utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=w04-launch-lu

## The story

What €300,000 and €1 million buy in six places, at the median price of homes sold in the 12 months to the second quarter of 2026 (INE):

- Lisbon, €5,192/m²: 58 m² for €300,000, 193 m² for €1 million
- Cascais, €4,889/m²: 61 m² and 205 m²
- The Algarve, in Loulé, €4,137/m²: 73 m² and 242 m²
- Porto, €3,631/m²: 83 m² and 275 m²
- Madeira, in Funchal, €3,538/m²: 85 m² and 283 m²
- Grândola, Comporta's municipality, €2,543/m² (municipality median): 118 m² and 393 m²

In 2016 the same €300,000 bought 160 m² in Lisbon, at the median of €1,875/m² in the first quarter of that year. Today's 58 m² is the other end of that line.

A median isn't a home. It's the middle of everything sold in a year, old and new, so it shows where your money buys more space, not what a given apartment costs. A municipality's median also doesn't describe the most sought-after spots inside it, such as the Comporta coast.

## The most-searched question

No reader questions yet, so we start with the most-searched one in our FAQ: can I buy a home in Portugal if I live abroad? Yes. You don't need to live in Portugal, and the IMT code even has its own rate for non-residents. You'll need a NIF, the Portuguese tax number, which you can get from Luxembourg through a representative. Because you live in the EU, a tax representative is optional once you own the home. You don't have to fly in for the deed either: a power of attorney signed at a Portuguese consulate, or before a Luxembourg notary with the Hague Apostille, does it.

A partner lawyer can take care of the NIF and the power of attorney: https://imoveisportugal.lu/en/lu/talk-to-a-partner/?cat=lawyer&utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=w04-launch-lu#form
