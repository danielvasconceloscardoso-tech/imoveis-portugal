This week's home

Our partner agency's first home goes here as soon as it sends one with permission to publish. Until then, this block runs the cost example: the bill for a €300,000 home if you aren't a Portuguese tax resident.

  • Price: €300,000
  • Transfer tax (IMT), 7.5% flat for non-residents: €22,500
  • Stamp duty, 0.8%: €2,400
  • Deed and registration at Casa Pronta: €375 (lawyer's fees are separate)
  • Total: €325,275, about R$1.84 million at the ECB reference rate of October 23, 2026 (R$5.6485 to the euro)

The law lists exceptions to the 7.5%, with the difference refunded on request: you become a Portuguese tax resident within two years, or you let the home at a capped rent within six months, for at least 36 of the first 60 months.

On the Brazilian side, if you're a tax resident in Brazil, the home goes under Bens e Direitos in your IRPF return. The euro price goes in the description, converted to dollars and then to reais at the Banco Central selling rate on the purchase date, so the declared figure won't match the ECB sum above. If your assets abroad add up to US$1 million or more on December 31, there's also the annual CBE declaration to the Banco Central. The story

Portugal house prices are easier to read as space. At the median price per m² of homes sold in the year to mid-2026 (INE), €300,000, about R$1.69 million, buys:

  • 58 m² in Lisbon
  • 61 m² in Cascais
  • 73 m² in Loulé, in the Algarve
  • 83 m² in Porto
  • 85 m² in Funchal, Madeira
  • 118 m² in Grândola, Comporta's municipality

That last one is the municipality median: homes by the Comporta beaches sell for more. Across the country, the same budget buys more than 100 m² in 281 of the 304 municipalities with an INE figure, and more than 200 m² in 189.

Ten years ago, at Porto's early-2016 median of €1,088 per m², the same money bought 276 m² in the city.

Medians aren't homes: they're the middle of everything sold in a year, old and new. They tell you where your money goes further, not what a given apartment costs.

The most-searched question

No reader questions yet. From Brazil, after "imt portugal", which the bill above answers, the top search is "morar em portugal", living in Portugal. The question behind it: does buying a home let me live in Portugal?

No. Since October 7, 2023 (Law 56/2023), buying property no longer opens new applications for the investment residence permit, the real-estate Golden Visa; permits already granted can be renewed. Buying and moving are separate processes. To live in Portugal, you need a residence visa, such as the one for people living on their own income (D7), or the CPLP visa, issued at a Portuguese consulate, which then leads to the CPLP residence permit from AIMA.
