## This week's home

Our partner agency's first home goes here from next week, with its permission. Until then, the bill for a €500,000 home bought by someone who isn't a Portuguese tax resident:

- Price: €500,000
- Transfer tax (IMT), 7.5% flat for non-residents: €37,500
- Stamp duty, 0.8%: €4,000
- Deed and registration at Casa Pronta: €375
- Total: €541,875, about $606,500 at the ECB rate of 23 October

There are exceptions to the 7.5%. The main one: you become a Portuguese tax resident within two years, and the difference is refunded on request.

## The story

Same budget, five places. $500,000 is about €446,700. At the median price of homes sold, that's 86 m² in Lisbon, 91 in Cascais, 108 in Loulé in the Algarve, 123 in Porto and 190 in Gondomar, next to Porto on the Douro.

Ten years ago the same euros bought 238 m² in Lisbon, at the 2016 median of €1,875. Today's 86 m² is the other end of that line.

Medians aren't homes: they're the middle of everything sold in a year, old and new. They tell you where your money goes further, not what a given apartment costs.

## The most-searched question

No reader questions yet, so we start with the one Americans search most: can I buy property in Portugal? Yes. There's no restriction on foreigners; the tax code even has its own IMT rate for non-resident buyers. You'll need a Portuguese tax number (NIF), which you can get from abroad. Once you own the home, you have 15 days to name a tax representative or sign up for the tax office's electronic notifications, and outside the EU that step isn't optional. You don't have to fly in for the deed: a power of attorney signed before a notary with the Hague Apostille, or at a Portuguese consulate, does it.

On the US side, a Portuguese bank account counts toward the $10,000 FBAR threshold.

A partner lawyer can take care of the NIF and the power of attorney: https://imoveisportugal.us/en/us/talk-to-a-partner/?cat=lawyer&utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=w04-launch-us#form
