## This week's home

Our partner agency's first home will run here as soon as we have its written permission. For now, here's what a €300,000 home costs if you aren't a Portuguese tax resident and don't take a mortgage:

- Price: €300,000
- Transfer tax (IMT), 7.5% flat for non-residents: €22,500
- Stamp duty, 0.8%: €2,400
- Deed and registration at Casa Pronta: €375
- Total cost with taxes, deed and registration: €325,275

The 7.5% has exceptions. If you become a Portuguese tax resident within two years of buying, you ask for the difference back. The law also lifts the flat rate for anyone who "has been considered a tax resident" in Portugal. If you lived in Portugal before moving to France, whether that covers you depends on your case: a question for a partner lawyer before the promissory contract.

Try it with your own price: https://imoveisportugal.fr/en/fr/calculators/total-cost/?price=300000&resident=0&utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=w04-launch-fr

## The story

Two budgets, six places. At the median price of homes sold in the 12 months to the second quarter of 2026 (INE), €300,000 and €1 million buy:

- Lisbon, €5,192/m²: 58 m² and 193 m²
- Cascais, €4,889/m²: 61 m² and 205 m²
- The Algarve, in Loulé, €4,137/m²: 73 m² and 242 m²
- Porto, €3,631/m²: 83 m² and 275 m²
- Madeira, in Funchal, €3,538/m²: 85 m² and 283 m²
- Grândola, Comporta's municipality, €2,543/m² (municipality median): 118 m² and 393 m²

Ten years ago the math was different. In the first quarter of 2016, Lisbon's median was €1,875/m², and the same €300,000 bought 160 m². Over those ten years, Lisbon's price per m² rose 177%.

A median isn't a home: it's the middle of everything sold in a year, new and old. It tells you where your money buys more space, not what a particular apartment costs. And a municipality's median doesn't describe the most sought-after spots inside it, such as the Comporta coast.

## The most-searched question

We don't have reader questions yet, so we start with the most-searched one in our FAQ: can I buy a home in Portugal if I live abroad? Yes, you can. The IMT code even has its own rate for non-residents. You'll need a NIF, the Portuguese tax number, which you can get from France through a representative. Because you live in the EU, naming a tax representative after the purchase is optional. For the deed, a power of attorney signed at the Portuguese consulate in Paris, or before a French notary with the Hague Apostille, saves you the trip.

On the French side, one rule to know: if you open a bank account in Portugal, you declare it every year with your tax return (form 3916), even if you don't use it.

A partner lawyer can take care of the NIF and the power of attorney: https://imoveisportugal.fr/en/fr/talk-to-a-partner/?cat=lawyer&utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=w04-launch-fr#form

## One number

€9,692. The median price of one square meter of an existing apartment in Paris buys about 1.9 m² in Lisbon, 2 in Cascais, 2.3 in Loulé, 2.7 in Porto and in Funchal, and 3.8 in Grândola.

*Method: median for existing apartments sold in Paris from October 2025 to September 2026 (Notaires de France), against the median of all homes sold in Portugal in the 12 months to June 2026 (INE).*
