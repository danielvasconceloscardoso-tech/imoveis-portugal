If you aren't a Portuguese tax resident, a €250,000 home costs €271,125 in all, and a €500,000 home costs €541,875. That is the price, the IMT transfer tax at the flat 7.5% rate the IMT code sets for non-residents (article 17, no. 10),[[pt-imt-nonresident-flat]] stamp duty of 0.8%[[pt-is-purchase]] and €375 for the deed and registration at a Casa Pronta office.[[pt-casa-pronta-fees]] Imóveis Portugal last checked the tax rates on October 24, 2026 and the Casa Pronta fees on October 25, 2026.

## Five lines make up the bill

The question we hear most is "How much will I pay in total, not just the price?" The answer has five lines, and a sixth with a mortgage.

1. **The price** agreed with the seller.
2. **IMT**, the transfer tax, paid once. Its rate depends on whether you're a Portuguese tax resident and, if you are, on how you'll use the home.
3. **Stamp duty on the purchase**, 0.8% of the price, the same for everyone.[[pt-is-purchase]]
4. **The deed and the registration.** At a Casa Pronta office both are done in one place for €375 with one registration act, or €700 with more than one, as when the purchase comes with a mortgage.[[pt-casa-pronta-fees]]
5. **The lawyer.** The fee is agreed directly with the lawyer, so it isn't in our examples.
6. **With a mortgage**, stamp duty of 0.6% on the amount borrowed (loans of five years or more)[[pt-is-loan-5y]] and 4% on the interest, paid over the life of the loan.[[pt-is-interest]]

## IMT is worked out in one of three ways

**Non-resident.** Non-resident buyers of an urban property used exclusively for housing pay IMT at a flat rate of 7.5%, with no exemption or reduction, unless an exception applies (IMT code, article 17, no. 10, added by Decree-Law 97/2026).[[pt-imt-nonresident-flat]] The exceptions: (a) you have been considered a Portuguese tax resident; (b) you become a tax resident within two years of the purchase; (c) the home is let for housing within six months, at a rent within the caps of Decree-Law 97/2026, for at least 36 months in the first five years. In (b) and (c) the difference is refunded on request within six months.[[pt-imt-nonresident-exceptions]] The rate, the exceptions and the open question on (a) are in our [article on the 7.5% for non-residents](/en/us/blog/7-5-percent-transfer-tax-non-residents/).

**Resident, own permanent home.** A progressive table applies.[[pt-imt-hpp-2026]] The price is split at the top of the bracket below it: the lower part pays that bracket's average rate, the excess pays the marginal rate. At €250,000, the first €198,347 pays 1.7274% (€3,426) and the remaining €51,653 pays 7% (€3,616): €7,042.

**Resident, other home** (a vacation home, for example). Same method, slightly higher table.[[pt-imt-second-home-2026]] At €250,000: €4,490 on the first €198,347 at 2.2636%, plus the same €3,616, so €8,105.

## The bill for a €500,000 home

Above €330,539 the 8% bracket applies to the excess. For your own home that means €12,680 on the first €330,539 at 3.8361%, plus €13,557 on the remaining €169,461.[[pt-imt-hpp-2026]] For another home, the first part pays 4.1578%.[[pt-imt-second-home-2026]]

## A mortgage adds two lines

For a home that isn't your own permanent residence, Banco de Portugal caps the loan at 80% of the value (90% for your own permanent home).[[pt-ltv-limits]] With an 80% loan on €250,000, you borrow €200,000: stamp duty on the loan is €1,200, and Casa Pronta charges €700 instead of €375 because the mortgage is registered too. The bill grows by €1,525, so a non-resident pays €272,650. At €500,000, the loan is €400,000, stamp duty €2,400, and the bill grows by €2,725 to €544,600. The 4% on interest comes later, with each payment.[[pt-is-interest]]

## IMT is due within 30 days of assessment

IMT is paid on the day it's assessed or within the following 30 days.[[pt-imt-payment]] If you expect a refund under exception (b) or (c), the full 7.5% is paid first and the difference is claimed afterwards.[[pt-imt-nonresident-exceptions]] For your own figure, the [total-cost calculator](/en/us/calculators/total-cost/?price=250000&resident=0) takes the price, residence, use and mortgage. Cost is step 5 of [your path](/en/us/your-path/?door=buy&step=5), and the [map](/en/us/map/?budget=250000) shows what a budget buys in square meters.

## The tax is the same from every country, the paperwork at home is not

### Luxembourg

The bill is in euros. Many readers in Luxembourg once lived in Portugal. Exception (a) reads "has been considered a Portuguese tax resident"; whether past residence qualifies is a question for a partner lawyer or tax adviser before you sign.[[pt-imt-nonresident-exceptions]]

### France

Also in euros. If you open a Portuguese account to pay, French tax residents declare every foreign account each year on form 3916.[[fr-3916]]

### Switzerland

€271,125 is about CHF 250,200 at the ECB rate of October 23, 2026.[[ecb-eur-chf]] Second-pillar money can be withdrawn early only for your main residence, in Switzerland or abroad, not for a second home, with a minimum of CHF 20,000.[[ch-2nd-pillar-abroad]] Outside the EU and EEA, owning property means naming a tax representative in Portugal or joining electronic notifications.[[pt-tax-rep]]

### Brazil

€271,125 is about R$1.53 million at the ECB rate of October 23, 2026.[[ecb-eur-brl]] The home goes in "Bens e Direitos" of the IRPF, valued in euros, converted to dollars and then to reais at the Banco Central rate on the purchase date.[[br-bens-exterior]] The tax representative rule applies.[[pt-tax-rep]]

### USA

€271,125 is about $303,500 at the ECB rate of October 23, 2026.[[ecb-eur-usd]] A Portuguese account used for the purchase counts toward the $10,000 FBAR threshold.[[us-fbar]] The tax representative rule applies.[[pt-tax-rep]]

### Israel

€271,125 is about ₪947,300 at the ECB rate of October 23, 2026.[[ecb-eur-ils]] The tax representative rule applies.[[pt-tax-rep]]

## A partner lawyer handles the purchase itself

Taxes are fixed by law; checking the property, the promissory contract and the deed is the lawyer's work. A [partner lawyer](/en/us/talk-to-a-partner/?cat=lawyer#form) can follow the purchase from start to finish, and a [partner tax adviser](/en/us/talk-to-a-partner/?cat=tax#form) can look at whether an IMT exception applies to you.

## Questions

**How much does it cost to buy a €250,000 home in Portugal if I live abroad?**

If you aren't a Portuguese tax resident, €271,125 in all: €18,750 IMT at the flat 7.5% non-resident rate of the IMT code, €2,000 stamp duty and €375 for the deed and registration at Casa Pronta. A mortgage adds 0.6% stamp duty on the loan and raises Casa Pronta to €700. The lawyer's fee is separate.

**Is IMT cheaper if I'm a Portuguese tax resident?**

Yes, at most prices. On €250,000, a resident pays €7,042 for their own permanent home and €8,105 for another home, against €18,750 for a non-resident. The resident tables are progressive, so the gap changes with the price. At €500,000 it's €26,237, €27,300 and €37,500.

**What does Casa Pronta cost?**

€375 for the deed and registration done in one place with one registration act, such as a purchase paid in cash. €700 when there's more than one act, such as a purchase with a mortgage, plus €50 for each additional property. Notary and lawyer fees, if you use them, are separate.

**Do I pay the 7.5% if I plan to move back to Portugal?**

You pay it at purchase. If you become a Portuguese tax resident within two years, the law lets you ask for the difference back within six months of becoming a resident. Whether past residence before you emigrated counts under exception (a) is still open: a partner lawyer or tax adviser can look at your case.
