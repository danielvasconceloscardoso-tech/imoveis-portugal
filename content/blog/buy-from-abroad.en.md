You can buy property in Portugal from abroad, and you don't need to be here on the day of the deed. People who live in other countries buy homes here, and the tax code even has its own IMT rate for them.[[pt-nonresidents-buy]]

The purchase follows a set order. Each step rests on an official rule, and this article goes through them one by one, from the first document to the keys.

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1. NIF | You get your Portuguese tax number |
| 2. Budget | You add taxes and fees to the price |
| 3. Mortgage | A bank looks at your loan request |
| 4. CPCV | You sign the promissory contract and pay a deposit |
| 5. Power of attorney | Someone you choose can sign for you |
| 6. Origin of funds | You show where the money comes from |
| 7. Taxes | You pay IMT and stamp duty |
| 8. Deed | You sign the deed and the home is registered |

## How to get a NIF from abroad

The NIF is your Portuguese tax number. Every purchase needs one, so it comes first.

You can request it online through a legal representative, or in person. You don't need a tax representative to get the NIF.[[pt-nif-nonresident]]

A second duty comes after the purchase. If you live outside the EU and the EEA (the European Economic Area), you must appoint a tax representative in Portugal. This is a person who deals with the tax authority on your behalf.

Inside the EU or the EEA, a tax representative is optional. Signing up for the tax authority's electronic notices also waives it. You have 15 days from the day you own property in Portugal.[[pt-tax-rep]]

## How much it costs to buy property in Portugal

The price is one line of your budget. The others are IMT, stamp duty and the deed. IMT is the transfer tax, paid once when you buy.

Stamp duty on the purchase is 0.8% of the price.[[pt-is-purchase]] With a loan of five years or more, there's also 0.6% on the amount you borrow.[[pt-is-loan-5y]] The bank then charges 4% stamp duty on the interest, over the life of the loan.[[pt-is-interest]]

IMT depends on the price, on how the home is used and on whether the buyer is a tax resident. For a non-resident it's 7.5% since May 2026, with exceptions. The [article on IMT for non-residents]({{post:imt-nonresidents}}) explains the rate and the exceptions.

On a €300,000 purchase by a non-resident with an 80% loan, the own funds needed come to €86,340: the down payment, the 7.5% IMT[[pt-imt-nonresident-flat]] and the stamp duty on the purchase and on the loan. You can run the same numbers with your own price in the [calculators]({{sims}}).

## How to get a mortgage as a non-resident

A Portuguese bank can lend to someone who lives abroad. Each bank decides case by case. Banco de Portugal sets limits that apply to everyone.

The loan can cover up to 90% of the value for a main home. For any other home, the limit is 80%. The value is the lower of the price and the bank's appraisal.[[pt-ltv-limits]]

The monthly payments on all your loans together shouldn't go above 45% of your net income.[[pt-dsti-45]] The longest term is 40 years up to age 35, and 35 years from that age.[[pt-maturity-limits]]

When the bank approves, it gives you the FINE. This is the European Standardised Information Sheet, with the terms and the draft contract. The offer binds the bank for at least 30 days. You can accept it only after the first seven days, the reflection period.[[pt-mortgage-fine-30-7]]

The bank may ask for insurance linked to the loan. It must accept a policy from another insurer if the cover is equivalent.[[pt-mortgage-insurance]]

## What the CPCV, the promissory contract, sets

The CPCV is the promissory purchase contract. You sign it before the deed. It sets the price, the date of the deed and the deposit, called the sinal.

The Civil Code sets clear rules for the deposit (article 442). If you back out through your own fault, you lose the deposit. If the seller backs out, you can claim double what you paid.[[pt-sinal-442]]

Many people have a lawyer read the CPCV before they sign it.

## How to sign without travelling to Portugal

You can sign every document from the country where you live. A power of attorney, the procuração, lets a person you choose sign for you in Portugal. Often that person is a lawyer.

You can sign the power of attorney at a Portuguese consulate. You can also sign it before a notary in your country. In that case it needs the Hague Apostille to be valid in Portugal.[[pt-poa-apostille]] The Apostille is an official certificate that confirms a foreign public document.

## Why the bank asks where the money comes from

Banks, notaries and lawyers in Portugal must ask about the origin of your funds when the risk profile or the transaction calls for it. The rule comes from the anti-money-laundering law, Law 83/2017.[[pt-aml-funds]]

Keeping your proof of funds at hand makes this step quicker.

## When to pay IMT

The taxes are paid before the deed. IMT is due on the day it's assessed or within the following 30 days.[[pt-imt-payment]] Your lawyer, or the office where the deed is done, confirms the deadline for each case.

## How the deed and the registration work

The escritura is the deed of sale, the final contract. At a Casa Pronta office, a desk of the Portuguese registry service, the deed and the registration happen in one place.

It costs €375 with one registration act, and €700 with a mortgage. Each extra property adds €50. Lawyer fees are separate.[[pt-casa-pronta-fees]]

For the appointment, the office asks for these documents:[[pt-casa-pronta-docs-all]]

- the civil and tax ID of sellers and buyers;
- the building's technical file (ficha técnica), if there is one;
- a deed that mentions the usage licence, or its waiver, depending on the construction date;
- the energy certificate.

## What you pay each year as an owner

Each year, the owner pays IMI, the municipal property tax. It's 0.3% to 0.45% of the home's tax value, at the rate the municipality sets.[[pt-imi-rates]]

Prices and rates change from one municipality to the next. The [map]({{map}}) shows home prices across the country. The questions that come up most after the purchase are in the [FAQ]({{faq}}).
