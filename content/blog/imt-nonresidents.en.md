IMT in Portugal is the transfer tax you pay once, when you buy a home. Since May 2026, buyers who aren't Portuguese tax residents pay a flat 7.5% transfer tax (IMT) on a home, with no exemption or reduction.[[pt-imt-nonresident-flat]]

There are three exceptions. Here is each one, with the numbers and the source.

Two words come up a lot. A tax resident is a person Portugal taxes as living here. Article 16 of the income tax code, the CIRS, sets the test. A flat rate is one rate on the whole price.

## What changed for non-residents in May 2026

The transfer tax (IMT) is what you pay when you buy property in Portugal. Until 2026 the rate depended on the price and on how the home would be used, and it was the same for residents and non-residents. Decree-Law 97/2026 added a rule to the IMT code: a buyer of an urban property or apartment for housing who isn't a Portuguese tax resident pays a flat 7.5%, with no exemption or reduction.[[pt-imt-nonresident-flat]]

An urban property is a house or other building, not farmland.

This applies if your tax home is in Luxembourg, France, Switzerland, Brazil, the US or Israel. Your starting point is then 7.5% of the price, unless an exception applies.

## When the 7.5% rate doesn't apply

The law lifts the flat rate in three situations.[[pt-imt-nonresident-exceptions]]

1. **The buyer has been considered a Portuguese tax resident**, under article 16 of the personal income tax code (CIRS).
2. **The buyer becomes a Portuguese tax resident within two years of the purchase.**
3. **The home is let as a residence within six months of the purchase**, at a monthly rent within the caps in Decree-Law 97/2026, for at least 36 months (consecutive or not) in the first five years.

In cases 2 and 3 the buyer pays 7.5% first, then asks the tax authority to refund the difference within six months of becoming a resident or signing the lease.

So in those two cases, you pay first. You get the difference back later. The tax authority is the Autoridade Tributária. Its website is the Portal das Finanças.

The first exception uses the words "has been considered a resident". Whether that covers people who lived in Portugal and then emigrated depends on how the law is read and on the person's case. That is a question for a lawyer or tax advisor before you sign the promissory contract.

The promissory contract, the CPCV, is the contract signed before the deed. It sets the price, the date and the deposit.

## How much IMT you pay as a non-resident

The table shows IMT only, using the 2026 tables. For residents, the rate depends on use: a main home (the buyer's own permanent residence) gets the lowest table, and another home, such as a vacation home, gets a slightly higher one.[[pt-imt-hpp-2026]][[pt-imt-second-home-2026]]

In Portuguese, a main home is called habitação própria e permanente.

| Home price | Resident, main home | Resident, other home | Non-resident |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| €250,000 | €7,042 | €8,105 | €18,750 |
| €400,000 | €18,237 | €19,300 | €30,000 |

Stamp duty is the same for everyone: 0.8% of the price, or €2,000 on a €250,000 home.[[pt-is-purchase]] It isn't in the table.

At €250,000, the gap between a non-resident and a resident buying a main home is €11,708. This gap is why the second exception matters if you plan to move to Portugal.

## When to pay IMT

IMT is due on the day it's assessed or within the following 30 days.[[pt-imt-payment]] Assessed means the tax authority has worked out the amount due.

If you'll ask for a refund under the second or third exception, plan to pay the full 7.5% at purchase.

## What you pay after IMT

IMT is paid once. After the purchase, the owner pays IMI every year, a municipal property tax of 0.3% to 0.45% of the tax value, at the rate each municipality sets.[[pt-imi-rates]] The tax value, the VPT, is the value the tax authority records for the home.

The country where you live may have its own rules about a home in Portugal, for example on rent.
