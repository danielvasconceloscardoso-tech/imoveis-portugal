If you aren't a Portuguese tax resident, IMT on a home is a flat 7.5% of the price. That's article 17(10) of the IMT Code, added by Decree-Law 97/2026: non-resident buyers of an urban property used exclusively for housing pay a flat rate, with no exemption or reduction, unless an exception applies.

**What it means in euros.** Residents pay IMT on a progressive table that depends on how they'll use the home; non-residents pay the flat rate whatever the price. With the 2026 tables:

- €250,000: €7,042 for a resident's own permanent home, €8,105 for a resident's other home, €18,750 for a non-resident.
- €300,000: €10,542, €11,605 and €22,500.
- €400,000: €18,237, €19,300 and €30,000.

The resident figures follow the splitting rule in article 17(3): the price is cut at the top of the bracket below it, the lower part pays that bracket's average rate and the rest pays the marginal rate. At €300,000 for your own home, €198,347 pays 1.7274% and the remaining €101,653 pays 7%.

**The three exceptions** (article 17(10) to (12)):

1. You have been considered a Portuguese tax resident.
2. You become a Portuguese tax resident within two years of the purchase.
3. You let the home for housing within six months, at a rent within the decree's caps, for at least 36 months in the first five years.

In cases 2 and 3 you pay the full 7.5% at purchase and the difference is refunded on request within six months. Case 1 is the one people who once lived in Portugal ask about; how it applies to past residence is still being read, so it's a question for a Portuguese lawyer or tax adviser with your own dates in hand.

**When it's paid.** On the day of assessment or within the following 30 days.

**What IMT isn't.** It's only one line of the bill. Stamp duty of 0.8% applies to everyone, and the deed with registration at a Casa Pronta office costs €375 (€700 with a mortgage). A €250,000 home therefore costs a non-resident €271,125 before the lawyer's fee.

Source: Código do IMT, art. 17.º (n.os 1, 3 and 10 to 12) and art. 36.º, on Portal das Finanças; Decreto-Lei 97/2026, Diário da República; Tabela Geral do Imposto do Selo, item 1.1; Justiça.gov.pt, Balcão Casa Pronta. Checked October 2026.
