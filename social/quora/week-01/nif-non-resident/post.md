You can get it without setting foot in Portugal. The NIF (número de identificação fiscal) is the Portuguese tax number, and the tax authority gives one to non-residents as well as residents.

According to the Autoridade Tributária's leaflet for non-resident foreigners, there are two ways to request it:

1. **Online, through a legal representative.** Someone you authorise, usually a lawyer or solicitador, files the request on the e-balcão of the Portal das Finanças. You send them your documents from wherever you live.
2. **In person,** at any Serviço de Finanças (tax office), if you happen to be in Portugal.

The same leaflet makes a point that trips many people up: you don't need a tax representative to get the NIF. The tax representative is a different role and a later obligation.

That later obligation is in article 19 of the Lei Geral Tributária. Once you own property in Portugal, you have 15 days to either appoint a tax representative or join the tax authority's electronic notification service. If you live outside the EU and the EEA (the US, Brazil, Switzerland or Israel, for example), one of the two is mandatory. If you live in an EU or EEA country, the representative is optional.

Why the NIF comes first: the deed can't be signed without it. At Casa Pronta, the public desk that handles the deed and the land registration together, the list of documents begins with the civil and tax identification of everyone signing.

Depending on where you live, there's also paperwork at home:

- **US persons:** a Portuguese bank account counts toward the FBAR, filed when foreign accounts together exceed $10,000 at any time in the year.
- **France:** every foreign account goes on form 3916 each year, even an unused one.
- **Brazil:** a property abroad goes into the IRPF under Bens e Direitos, converted from euros to dollars and then to reais.

The official sources I checked don't state a processing time or a fee for the online route, and each representative charges their own, so the representative is the one who can quote both. For anything specific to your situation, such as having been a Portuguese tax resident before, a lawyer in Portugal is the person to ask.
