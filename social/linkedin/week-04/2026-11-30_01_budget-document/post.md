Portugal's median square metre: €2,264. Up 17.7% in a year.

That's INE's figure for homes sold in the 12 months to June 2026, released on October 23. A year earlier it was €1,923.

We read it as space, because that's how people abroad ask the question. At the median, €300,000 buys more than 100 m² in 281 of the 304 municipalities with an INE figure. In Lisbon, about 58 m². In Porto, 83. In Grândola, Comporta's municipality, 118.

The document puts it on nine pages: the national line since 2016 (+173%), what €500,000 buys in six places, what a non-resident pays to close, and the gross yield INE's rent data gives (5.2% nationally, before costs and tax).

Medians aren't homes. They're the middle of everything sold in a year, new and existing, and they don't describe prime areas inside a municipality. They show where a budget goes further.

From December 1, the same figures sit on one map, for readers in Luxembourg, France, Switzerland, Brazil, the US and Israel.
